## What a warning sign does

::: law vmf 2:4
> Varningsmärken varnar för en fara och upplyser om farans art. Varningsmärken är uppsatta på det avstånd från faran som gör dem mest effektiva såväl på dagen som på natten.
>
> Om ett varningsmärke används för att ange en fara som sträcker sig över en längre vägsträcka är sträckans längd angiven på en tilläggstavla. Om det behövs kan märket vara upprepat på lämpliga avstånd.

**In English:** "Warning signs warn of a danger and tell you what kind of danger it is. They are placed at the distance from the danger that makes them most effective both by day and by night. If a warning sign is used for a danger that continues along a longer stretch of road, the length of the stretch is given on an additional panel. If needed, the sign can be repeated at suitable distances."
:::

Almost all warning signs are **yellow triangles with a red border and the point up**. A warning sign does not forbid anything by itself. It tells you that something ahead needs your attention, so you can **slow down, look and get ready in time**.

## How far before the danger?

Transportstyrelsen's regulations set the distance from the sign to the danger. It depends on the speed limit [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:2]]:

| Speed limit on the road | Sign stands this far before the danger |
|---|---|
| 50 km/h or lower | 5–75 metres |
| 60 or 70 km/h | 50–200 metres |
| 80 or 90 km/h | 150–250 metres |
| Higher than 90 km/h | 200–400 metres |

If a sign has to stand further away than this, the distance is shown on additional panel [[sign:T2|T2, distance]] [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:2]]. If the danger goes on for a while – for example several bends or a stretch with animals – panel [[sign:T1|T1]] shows how long the stretch is [[src:vmf|2:4]].

::: warning No sign does not mean no danger
On roads with the **base speed** of 70 km/h ([[term:bashastighet]]), warning signs may only be put up for things road users **cannot be expected to foresee**. On other roads they are put up when a danger is **hard to discover in time** at a legal speed [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:1]]. Ordinary dangers, such as a normal bend, a junction you can see, or ice in winter, are often **not** signed. You must always adapt your speed to what you can see [[src:tf|3:14]].
:::

## The road ahead – shape and surface

[[signs:A1-1,A1-2,A2-1,A2-2,A3,A4,A5,A6,A7,A8]]

- **[[sign:A1|A1 dangerous bend]]** and **[[sign:A2|A2 several dangerous bends]]** show which way the (first) bend turns. They mark bends that are sharper than the road suggests [[src:vmf|2:5]]. Brake **before** the bend, not in it.
- **[[sign:A3|A3 steep descent]]** and **[[sign:A4|A4 steep ascent]]** give the slope in per cent. 10 % means the road drops or climbs 10 metres per 100 metres [[src:vmf|2:5]]. Downhill, use a lower gear so the engine helps to brake.
- **[[sign:A5|A5 road narrows]]** – the symbol is drawn to match the narrowing. Where a wide road narrows to 5.5 m or less, panel T4 shows the free width [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:5]].
- **[[sign:A6|A6 opening bridge]]** – a bridge that can open, for example for boats.
- **[[sign:A7|A7 quay]]** – the road ends at water. At a ferry berth the sign has a panel with the word *Färja* (ferry) [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:6]].
- **[[sign:A8|A8 uneven road]]** – bumps or damage that make it wise to drive slower than you otherwise would [[src:vmf|2:5]].

[[signs:A9,A10,A11,A12-1,A24-1,A26,A27]]

- **[[sign:A9|A9 speed bump]]** – a hump or dip built on purpose to slow traffic down.
- **[[sign:A10|A10 slippery road]]** – the road can be slippery. The sign is used for **snow or ice only if there are special reasons** [[src:vmf|2:5]], so in winter you must expect ice without any sign.
- **[[sign:A11|A11 loose stones]]** – risk of stones being thrown up. Not used on gravel roads, where this is normal [[src:vmf|2:5]]. Keep a longer distance and lower speed.
- **[[sign:A12|A12 falling rocks]]** – stones can fall onto the road from a cliff.
- **[[sign:A24|A24 side wind]]** – strong wind from the side is common. It hits hard on bridges, when leaving a forest, and when you pass a large vehicle. Hold the steering wheel or handlebars firmly.
- **[[sign:A26|A26 tunnel]]**.
- **[[sign:A27|A27 soft verge or high road edge]]** – keep the wheels on the carriageway.

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** A10, A11 and A24 matter more on a motorcycle. On a slippery or gravelly surface, brake and lean gently and avoid sudden changes. In side wind, stay relaxed on the handlebars and leave space to the side of your lane.
:::

## People on or near the road

[[signs:A13,A14,A15,A16,A17,A18]]

- **[[sign:A13|A13 pedestrian crossing ahead]]** – warns of a crossing you may not see in time. Not used at crossings with traffic lights that are already warned of with A22 [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:7]].
- **[[sign:A14|A14 pedestrians]]**, **[[sign:A15|A15 children]]**, **[[sign:A16|A16 cyclists and moped riders]]**, **[[sign:A17|A17 skiers]]** and **[[sign:A18|A18 horse riders]]** mark places where these road users often **cross or stay on or beside the road** [[src:vmf|2:5]].

The law already tells you to keep a low enough speed when you approach **children** on or beside the road, at pedestrian crossings and wherever people cross [[src:tf|3:15]]. The sign just tells you that this is such a place.

## Animals

[[signs:A19-1,A19-2,A19-3,A19-4,A19-5,A19-6,A19-7]]

**[[sign:A19|A19 animals]]** marks a place or stretch with a special risk of animals on or beside the road [[src:vmf|2:5]]. The basic sign shows an **elk** (*älg* – called a moose in North America). Other approved symbols are deer, cattle, horse, reindeer, sheep and wild boar [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:8]]. The [[lesson:wildlife]] lesson explains how to avoid hitting animals and what to do if it happens.

## Junctions and other traffic

[[signs:A22,A25,A28,A29-1,A30,A31,A32-1,A33,A34]]

- **[[sign:A22|A22 traffic signals ahead]]** – a junction or crossing controlled by traffic lights with three lights. In built-up areas it is only used if there are special reasons [[src:vmf|2:5]].
- **[[sign:A25|A25 two-way traffic]]** – a one-way carriageway turns into a road with traffic in both directions. It must be used where the limit is above 50 km/h [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:11]].
- **[[sign:A28|A28 crossroads]]** – a junction where the **right-hand rule** applies: give way to vehicles from your right [[src:vmf|2:5]] [[src:tf|3:18]].
- **[[sign:A29|A29 junction where side roads must give way]]** – you are on the road with priority; drivers on the side roads have to give way or stop. The wide line in the symbol is your road [[src:vmf|2:5]]. On a main road it is put up before junctions that are complicated or hard to see [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:12]].
- **[[sign:A30|A30 roundabout ahead]]**.
- **[[sign:A31|A31 slow-moving vehicles]]** (such as tractors), **[[sign:A32|A32 horse-drawn vehicles]]** and **[[sign:A33|A33 snowmobiles]]** mark places where they often cross or enter the road.
- **[[sign:A34|A34 queues likely]]** – a stretch where queues often form. Keep your distance and be ready to stop.

::: tip A28 or A29?
Both show a junction. **A28** has an **X**: nobody has priority by signs, so the right-hand rule applies. **A29** shows **your road as a wide line**: the others have to give way to you. See [[lesson:priority-signs]].
:::

## Road works, accidents and other dangers

[[signs:A20,A21,A23-1,A40,A41]]

- **[[sign:A20|A20 road works]]** – a stretch with road works or similar work. It stands on **both sides** of the road, unless it is obvious that you are approaching road works [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:9]]. Expect workers, machines, narrow lanes, loose gravel and temporary speed limits.
- **[[sign:A21|A21 end of road works]]** – only used when the end is not clear anyway [[src:vmf|2:5]].
- **[[sign:A23|A23 low-flying aircraft]]** – near an airfield. A panel with the word *Signal* tells you there are traffic signals that can stop you [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:10]].
- **[[sign:A40|A40 other danger]]** – a danger that no other warning sign covers. A panel tells you what it is [[src:vmf|2:5]], for example *Utryckning* (emergency vehicles exit) [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:21]].
- **[[sign:A41|A41 accident]]** – put up only at a traffic accident that blocks part of the road, and only for as long as it does [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:22]]. By law you must keep a low enough speed past an accident [[src:tf|3:15]].

## Railways and tramways

[[signs:A35,A36,A37,A38-1,A38-2,A38-3,A39-1,A39-3]]

- **[[sign:A35|A35 level crossing with barriers]]**, **[[sign:A36|A36 level crossing without barriers]]** and **[[sign:A37|A37 tramway crossing without barriers]]** warn of a crossing ahead [[src:vmf|2:5]].
- **[[sign:A38|A38 distance markers]]** count down to the crossing in **thirds**: three stripes, then two, then one [[src:vmf|2:5]]. Three stripes are used with A35–A37 when those stand at least 150 m before the crossing [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:17]].
- **[[sign:A39|A39 St Andrew's cross]]** marks a crossing with **one or more tracks** and stands **immediately before** it – normally at least five metres before the nearest rail [[src:vmf|2:5]] [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:20]]. Where there is more than one track, another train can come as soon as one has passed.

The full rules for level crossings – including the signals and barriers – are in [[lesson:railway-crossings]].

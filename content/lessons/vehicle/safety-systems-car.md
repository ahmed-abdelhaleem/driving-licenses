The curriculum requires you to explain the car's **protection systems**, how they work, and why it matters to **use** them [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:2]]. Cars have two kinds:

- **Passive safety** – protects you **in** a crash: seat belts, airbags, head restraints, the car body.
- **Active safety** – helps **avoid** the crash: ABS, ESC, emergency braking, lane-keeping and more.

## Seat belts

The seat belt is the most important protection in the car. According to Trafikverket's research summary, the belt reduced deaths among front-seat occupants of cars by about **45 %** [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. It holds you in place, spreads the forces over the strong parts of your body, and keeps you from being thrown out or into other people.

Using it is the law for everyone, with few exceptions – see [[lesson:passengers-and-loads]] [[src:tf|4:10]]. Most cars have a **seat belt reminder**; studies cited by Trafikverket show that belt use in the front seats rises to about **99 %** in cars with an effective reminder [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]].

::: warning A loose belt is a bad belt
Wear the belt **tight**, with the lap part **low over the hips** and the shoulder part over the **middle of the shoulder** – never under the arm or behind the back. Thick winter jackets make the belt loose.
:::

## Airbags

Airbags inflate in a fraction of a second in a collision and catch the head and body. Trafikverket's summary reports that a driver airbag reduced deaths in **frontal** crashes by about **29 %**, and **belt and airbag together** by about **61 %** in frontal crashes [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. An airbag is made to work **with** the belt – without the belt it can injure you.

- Sit at a normal distance from the steering wheel, and keep your hands on the outside of the wheel.
- **Never** place a child seat – or a child in a booster – in front of an **active** passenger airbag. A child is not safe in front of an active airbag until about **140 cm** tall [[src:ts-children]].
- **Side airbags and curtains** protect in side impacts, and are no danger to children in child seats [[src:ts-children]].

## Head restraints

A correctly adjusted **head restraint** – high enough and close to the back of your head – protects your neck in a **rear-end collision**, where neck injuries (*whiplash*) are common. Adjust it for every person in the car.

## Electronic assistance systems

| System | What it does |
|---|---|
| **ABS** | Stops the wheels locking when you brake hard, so you can steer. See [[lesson:brakes-and-abs]]. |
| **ESC** (anti-skid) | Brakes single wheels to stop a skid. See [[lesson:skids-and-traction]]. |
| **FCW** – collision warning | Warns when you are about to run into a vehicle or road user ahead [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. |
| **AEB** – automatic emergency braking | Brakes by itself to avoid or reduce a collision with vehicles – and in some cars also pedestrians and cyclists [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. |
| **LDW** – lane departure warning | Warns when the car is about to leave its lane, generally from about 65 km/h [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. |
| **LKA** – lane keeping assist | Steers to keep the car in its lane, generally from about 50–65 km/h [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. |
| **ISA** – intelligent speed assistance | Shows the speed limit and helps you stay within it. Standard equipment in all **new cars in the EU from 2024** [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. |

Trafikverket's summary reports clear safety effects: for example, lane departure warning reduced injuries in single-vehicle and head-on crashes by about **40 %** in Swedish data, and collision warning plus automatic braking reduced injury rear-end collisions by about **half** [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]].

::: key You are still the driver
Assistance systems work only in certain conditions – many only above or below certain speeds [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]], and their cameras and sensors can be blocked by snow, dirt or low sun. **You** are always responsible for driving the car [[src:tf|3:14]]. Learn what your car has and how it behaves – the owner's manual explains it.
:::

## Choosing a safe car

Trafikverket's basic safety advice includes **using a safe car** [[src:tv-car-safety]]. The European **Euro NCAP** programme gives cars up to **five stars** after tests of protection for adults, children and vulnerable road users, and of assistance systems. Research cited by Trafikverket found that the risk of serious and fatal injury in collisions between cars was clearly lower in **5-star** cars than in 2-star cars [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]].

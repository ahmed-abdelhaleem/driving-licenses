## No clutch, two pedals

An automatic car changes gear by itself. There is **no clutch pedal** – only the **accelerator** and the **brake**, both worked by the **right foot**. Your left foot rests on the floor.

::: warning Never brake with the left foot
Using both feet can make you press the brake and accelerator at the same time, or brake far too hard in a surprise. Keep the left foot still.
:::

## The selector

| Position | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Park | The gearbox is **locked**. Select it only when the car is **standing still**, when you park. |
| **R** | Reverse | Driving backwards. Select it only when the car is **standing still**. |
| **N** | Neutral | No drive to the wheels. For example when the car is towed or pushed. |
| **D** | Drive | Normal driving forwards. The gearbox chooses the gear. |

Many cars also have **M** (manual gear choice, often with + and –), **S** (sport) or **L / B / 1–2** (lower gears for **engine braking**). The owner's manual explains your car's positions.

Usually you must **press the brake pedal** to move the selector out of P, and some cars have a button on the lever. Many newer cars use buttons or a dial instead of a lever.

## Moving off and stopping

::: steps
1. **Foot on the brake.** Start the car.
2. Select **D** (or **R**) while you keep braking.
3. **Check** mirrors and blind spot, signal if needed, release the parking brake.
4. **Release the brake** – the car starts to **creep** forwards slowly by itself.
5. **Give gas** gently to drive.
:::

To stop, simply brake. You can stay in **D** at traffic lights with your foot on the brake.

::: key Creeping
Most automatics **creep** forwards in D or backwards in R without gas. This is useful when parking or in queues – but it also means the car **moves as soon as you release the brake**. Keep your foot on the brake whenever you stand still in D or R.
:::

## Overtaking: kick-down

If you press the accelerator **fully down**, the gearbox changes down quickly to give maximum power – called **kick-down**. Useful when you need to overtake or join fast traffic, but plan ahead so you do not depend on it.

## Engine braking in an automatic

An automatic gives less engine braking in D than a manual car in a low gear. On a **long, steep descent**, select a **lower range** (for example **L**, **B**, **2** or use **M** with –) so the engine helps hold the speed and the brakes do not overheat. See sign [[sign:A3|A3]].

## Parking

Stop with the brake, select **P**, and apply the **parking brake** – especially on a slope. Then switch off. Many cars apply the electric parking brake automatically.

::: warning P is not a brake
P locks the gearbox with a small pin. On a steep slope, put on the **parking brake before** you select P and release the foot brake, so the weight of the car rests on the brake and not on the pin.
:::

## Hybrid and electric cars

**Electric cars** do not have a gearbox with several gears – they are driven like automatics, with a selector for D, R, P and N. Many can brake by **regenerating energy** when you lift off the accelerator. See [[lesson:electric-and-hybrid]].

## Automatic test = code 78

If you take the driving test in an automatic car, your licence gets **code 78** in field 12 – you may only drive cars with automatic transmission [[src:ts-card]]. With code 78 you can still drive automatic and electric cars of any make. See [[lesson:code-78]] for how to remove the code later.

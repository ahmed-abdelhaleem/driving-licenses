## The controls at a glance

| Control | Where | Worked by |
|---|---|---|
| **Throttle** (twist grip) | Right handlebar | Right hand – twist towards you for more power |
| **Front brake lever** | Right handlebar | Right hand (usually two or more fingers) |
| **Clutch lever** | Left handlebar | Left hand |
| **Gear lever** | Left side, by the footrest | Left foot |
| **Rear brake pedal** | Right side, by the footrest | Right foot |

Most motorcycles use the gear pattern **1 – N – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5 (– 6)**: press the lever **down** for first gear, and lift it **up** for the others. **Neutral** sits between first and second – a half step. A green **N** light shows neutral. Some motorcycles and most scooters are **automatic** – then there is no clutch lever or gear lever.

::: note At the riding test
The motorcycle must have a **manual gearbox and a clutch lever** – unless the test is for an automatic-only licence [[src:tv-mc]].
:::

## Switches

- **Kill switch** (engine stop switch), red, on the right handlebar – stops the engine quickly, for example after a fall. It is part of the safety check at the test [[src:tv-mc]].
- **Starter button.**
- **Indicators** (left handlebar). On many motorcycles they do **not cancel themselves** – switch them off after the manoeuvre, or you send false signals.
- **Horn.**
- **Dipped and main beam**, often with a "flash" button. New models of motorcycles have **lights that switch on automatically** under EU rules [[src:eu-168-2013]].
- **Hazard lights** on many newer motorcycles.

## Mirrors and shoulder checks

Mirrors on a motorcycle often vibrate and show less than a car's. **Shoulder checks** – turning your head to look into the blind spot – are even more important on a motorcycle before changing lanes, turning or pulling out.

## Moving off and changing gear

::: steps
1. Engine running, **clutch lever pulled in**, press the gear lever **down** into **first**.
2. Check mirrors and shoulder, signal if you pull out.
3. Open the throttle a little and **slowly release the clutch** to the **biting point** – the motorcycle starts to move.
4. Release the clutch fully as you gain speed, with both feet up on the footrests.
5. **Changing up:** close the throttle, clutch in, lift the gear lever firmly, clutch out, open the throttle.
6. **Changing down:** clutch in, press the lever down, release the clutch smoothly – with a blip of throttle the change becomes softer.
:::

## Low speed: clutch, throttle, rear brake

At walking pace, balance is hardest. The manoeuvre test starts with a **low-speed part** where you push the motorcycle with the engine off, then crawl through a course with turns, including **stopping and starting while turning**. The speed is mainly controlled with the **clutch**, supported by throttle and brake [[src:tv-mc]]. The curriculum calls this **good balance when crawling** [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]].

::: tip Crawling technique
Keep a little steady throttle, control the speed by **slipping the clutch** at the biting point, and steady the motorcycle with **light rear brake**. Look **ahead** to where you want to go – not down at the ground. Avoid the front brake when the handlebars are turned at low speed – it can tip the motorcycle over.
:::

## Getting to know your motorcycle

The curriculum requires you to explain the motorcycle's **construction and functions** and how they affect safety and the environment [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]]. Read the owner's manual: tyre pressures (solo and with passenger), chain care, the meaning of each warning light, and how ABS and any traction control work.

## Why a car needs gears

An engine only works well within a certain range of speeds (revolutions per minute, **rpm**). The **gearbox** lets the engine run at a good speed whether the car is crawling or doing 110 km/h:

- **Low gears** (1st, 2nd) give **strong pulling power** but a low top speed – for moving off and climbing.
- **High gears** (5th, 6th) give **low engine speed** at high road speed – quiet and economical.
- **Reverse** (R) drives the car backwards.
- **Neutral** (N) – no gear engaged; the engine is disconnected from the wheels.

The gear pattern is usually drawn on the gear knob. Most cars have five or six forward gears.

## The clutch

The **clutch** (*koppling*) connects and disconnects the engine and the gearbox:

- **Pedal up** – engine and wheels are connected.
- **Pedal fully down** – they are disconnected, so you can change gear or stand still with the engine running.
- **Biting point** (*frikopplingspunkten*) – the point as you lift the pedal where the clutch starts to grip and the car wants to move. Learning to find it smoothly is the key to moving off.

::: warning Do not "ride" the clutch
Do not rest your foot on the clutch pedal while driving, and do not hold the car still on a hill by slipping the clutch. Both wear out the clutch quickly. Use the brake or the parking brake instead.
:::

## Moving off

::: steps
1. **Clutch down**, select **1st gear**.
2. **Check** mirrors and blind spot, and **signal** if you pull out from the kerb.
3. Give a **little gas** and lift the clutch slowly to the **biting point** – the engine sound drops slightly and the car wants to move.
4. **Release the parking brake** (or move your right foot from the brake to the accelerator).
5. Lift the clutch the rest of the way **smoothly** while giving a little more gas.
:::

### Starting on a hill

At the driving test you must be able to start uphill or downhill [[src:tv-driving-b]]. Uphill, the car rolls back if you release the brake before the clutch bites:

1. Hold the car with the **parking brake** (or the foot brake).
2. Find the **biting point** with a little more gas than on the flat.
3. When you feel the car pulling against the brake, **release the parking brake** and drive off.

Many modern cars have a **hill-start assist** that holds the brake for a moment. Learn to start without it too.

## Changing gear

**Changing up:** ease off the gas, **clutch down**, move the lever to the next gear, lift the clutch **smoothly**, give gas.

**Changing down:** used before you need more power (overtaking, uphill) or when you slow down. Clutch down, select the lower gear, lift the clutch smoothly – a little gas while lifting gives a softer change.

::: tip Driving economically
Change up **early**, at fairly low engine speed, and drive in the **highest gear** that the engine pulls without struggling. This saves fuel and reduces emissions – the curriculum requires a driving technique that gives **low fuel consumption** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:2]]. More in [[lesson:eco-driving]].
:::

## Engine braking

When you take your foot off the gas **with a gear engaged**, the engine slows the car down – **engine braking** (*motorbroms*). The lower the gear, the stronger the effect.

- On a **long, steep descent**, choose a lower gear so the engine helps you. The brakes then do not overheat. See sign [[sign:A3|A3]].
- When you slow down for a junction, you can stay in gear with the clutch up until the engine speed gets low, and then press the clutch before the engine stalls.

::: warning Do not coast in neutral
Driving with the gear in **neutral** or the **clutch pressed down** (coasting) **removes engine braking**, lets the speed downhill increase quickly, makes you use the footbrake more (which can reduce its effect), affects the steering in bends, and makes it harder to select the right gear when you need it [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 122]].
:::

## Stopping

Brake smoothly. Press the **clutch down** just before the car stops, so the engine does not stall. When stopped for a while, select **neutral** and use the parking brake if needed.

When you park, leave the car **in gear** (1st or reverse) and apply the **parking brake**, so it cannot roll [[src:tf|3:50]].

## A licence for both

If you pass the driving test in a **manual** car, your licence lets you drive both manual and automatic cars. If you pass in an automatic, you get code 78 – automatic only. See [[lesson:code-78]].

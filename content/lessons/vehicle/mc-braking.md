## Two brakes – used together

A motorcycle has a **front brake** (lever, right hand) and a **rear brake** (pedal, right foot). The curriculum requires you to use **different ways to brake a motorcycle** [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]], and the riding test ends with **efficient and controlled braking** to a stop from **[[fact:drivingTest.mcBrakeSpeeds]]**, using **both the front brake and the rear brake**. The examiner looks at your riding position, braking technique and how quickly you stop [[src:tv-mc]].

## Why the front brake does most of the work

When you brake, the motorcycle's weight moves **forward**: the front tyre is pressed harder into the road and can transfer much more braking force, while the rear tyre gets **lighter** and locks easily. The **front brake** gives about **three-quarters** of the braking power, so you must use **both** brakes to stop quickly [[src:sgi-mc|Braking]]. The rear brake adds stability and the rest of the force.

::: key Squeeze, do not grab
Apply the brakes with a **progressive squeeze** [[src:sgi-mc|Braking]]: a quick first light pressure lets the weight move forward and the front suspension compress, then **squeeze harder** – all within a fraction of a second. **Grabbing** the lever suddenly can lock the front wheel before the weight has moved.
:::

## Emergency stop

::: steps
1. **Close the throttle** completely.
2. **Front brake:** squeeze progressively and hard. **Rear brake:** press firmly at the same time [[src:sgi-mc|Quick stops]].
3. **Clutch in** as the speed drops, so the engine does not stall [[src:sgi-mc|Quick stops]].
4. **Keep the motorcycle upright** and look **ahead**, far forward – not down at the road.
5. Arms slightly bent, grip the tank with your knees, so your body does not slide forward onto the handlebars.
6. When you have stopped, put a foot down and keep a brake on so the motorcycle cannot roll.
:::

## ABS on a motorcycle

**ABS** stops the wheels from locking. On a motorcycle this is especially important: a locked **front** wheel can quickly make the motorcycle fall. Under EU rules, new motorcycles **over 125 cc** must have ABS; for smaller motorcycles the manufacturer may choose between ABS and a combined brake system [[src:eu-168-2013]]. Trafikverket lists **ABS – also on light motorcycles – and cornering ABS** among the important safety measures for riders [[src:tv-mc-safety]].

- With ABS: in an emergency, brake **hard and progressively** with both brakes and **keep the pressure** – the ABS releases a wheel that is about to lock.
- Without ABS: apply both brakes with a progressive squeeze, as hard as you can **without locking** the wheels. If the **rear** wheel does lock, do **not** release the brake until you have come to a complete stop [[src:sgi-mc|Quick stops]].
- **Combined brakes** (CBS) link the front and rear brakes, so one lever or pedal also applies the other brake.
- **Cornering ABS** also works when the motorcycle is leaned over, within limits.

## Braking in a bend

Grip is shared between cornering and braking. Braking hard while leaning uses grip you need to stay in the bend, and braking makes the motorcycle **stand up** and run wide. So:

- Do your braking **before** the bend, upright [[src:tf|3:14]] [[src:sgi-mc|Braking]].
- If you must brake in the bend, brake **gently and progressively** with both brakes – or, if there is room, **straighten up first** and then brake hard.

## Braking on poor surfaces

On gravel, wet road markings, leaves or wet steel (manhole covers, tram rails), grip is low – brake **earlier, softer and upright**. See [[lesson:mc-road-surface]].

## Practise

Practise emergency stops regularly on a closed, safe area – from low speeds first. The riding test requires efficient stops from 70 and 90 km/h [[src:tv-mc]], and the reaction you build in practice is what you will use on the road.

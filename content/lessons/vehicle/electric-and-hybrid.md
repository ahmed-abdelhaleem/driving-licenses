## Kinds of electrified cars

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Battery electric car** (BEV, *elbil*) | Only an electric motor and a large battery, charged from the grid. No exhaust at all from the car. |
| **Hybrid** (HEV, *elhybrid*) | A combustion engine plus a small electric motor and battery charged while driving and braking. Cannot be plugged in. |
| **Plug-in hybrid** (PHEV, *laddhybrid*) | A combustion engine plus an electric motor with a battery you can **charge from the grid**, so shorter trips can be driven on electricity. |
| **Fuel-cell car** (*vätgasbil*) | Makes electricity from hydrogen on board. |

Most electric cars have **no clutch pedal and no gears to change**, so they are driven like an automatic. Transportstyrelsen counts a vehicle with an automatic gearbox without a clutch pedal as automatic [[src:ts-faq]] – so if you pass the driving test in such a car, you get **code 78**, automatic only [[src:tv-theory-b]]. See [[lesson:code-78]].

## How an electric car drives

- **Instant power.** The full pulling force is there from standstill. It is easy to accelerate faster than you meant – especially on slippery roads, where the wheels can spin. Be gentle with the accelerator.
- **Weight.** Batteries are heavy, so many electric cars weigh more than similar petrol cars. More weight means more energy in a collision and can mean a longer braking distance – keep a good distance.
- **Regenerative braking.** When you lift your foot off the accelerator, the motor works as a generator and slows the car while charging the battery. Some cars can almost stop on this alone ("one-pedal driving"). Brake lights may come on when regeneration is strong – but use the **foot brake** whenever you need to stop properly.
- **Silence.** At low speed an electric car is very quiet. Pedestrians – especially children, older people and people with impaired sight – may not hear you coming. Drive slowly in car parks and residential areas and watch for people.

## Range and winter

The **range** depends on speed, temperature, heating and load. In **cold** weather the battery gives less energy and heating the car uses more, so the range can drop noticeably. Plan charging stops on long winter trips, and pre-heat the car while it is still connected to the charger if you can.

## Charging places

[[signs:E19,T24]]

A **charging place** (*laddplats*) is shown by the parking sign with panel [[sign:T24|T24]] [[src:vmf|2:30]].

::: law tf 3:54
> På en laddplats får endast fordon som kan laddas externt med elektrisk energi för fordonets framdrivning parkeras eller stannas. Andra fordon får inte parkeras eller stannas annat än för på- eller avstigning.

**In English:** "At a charging place, only vehicles that can be charged externally with electrical energy for their propulsion may be parked or stopped. Other vehicles may not be parked or stopped except to let people get in or out."
:::

So a **petrol car** – or an ordinary **hybrid** that cannot be plugged in – may not park at a charging place. Panels can add more conditions, for example a time limit.

## Environmental zones

In **environmental zone class 3**, the strictest class, cars are basically only allowed if they are **electric**, **fuel-cell** or certain **gas** vehicles [[src:tf|4:22d]]. See [[lesson:special-areas]].

## Coming changes

Under the EU's new driving licence directive, a B licence holder who has had the licence for two years will be able to drive cars running fully or partly on **alternative fuels** – for example electricity – up to **4,250 kg**, from **26 November 2027** (not motorhomes) [[src:ts-future]]. Until then, the normal 3,500 kg limit for B applies.

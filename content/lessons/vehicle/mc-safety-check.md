## What can be checked at the test

The riding test starts with a check of a selection of these [[src:tv-mc]]:

- lights, direction indicators, reflectors and the horn,
- brakes,
- tyres and wheel rims,
- power transmission (chain, belt or shaft),
- the stand,
- mirrors,
- bearings (steering head, swingarm and wheel bearings),
- suspension and shock absorbers,
- engine oil level,
- the emergency stop (kill) switch, if there is one.

If you find a fault, you should be able to suggest what to do [[src:tv-mc]]. The curriculum also requires you to **carry out simple checks and suggest measures** so the motorcycle is roadworthy [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]].

## How to check

### Lights, indicators, reflectors and horn

Switch on the ignition and check **dipped and main beam**, **rear light**, **number-plate light**, **all indicators**, and the **brake light** – it must come on both with the **front brake lever** and the **rear brake pedal**. Check that **reflectors** are whole and press the **horn**.

### Brakes

- **Levers and pedal** – firm, not spongy, and not touching the handlebar or the footrest.
- **Brake fluid** in the reservoirs between MIN and MAX.
- **Pads** – enough material left (look into the caliper if you can).
- **Discs** – no deep grooves or cracks.
- **Test:** push the motorcycle and brake with each brake on its own – it should stop at once.

### Tyres and rims

Look for **cuts, cracks, bulges and objects** stuck in the tread, check the **tread depth** over the whole tread, and check the **pressure** when the tyres are cold, against the owner's manual – it is often different with a passenger. Check the **rims** for dents and cracks, and the **spokes** if it has them. A motorcycle has only two small contact patches – a tyre fault can make you fall.

### Chain, belt or shaft

- **Chain:** check the **slack** (how much it moves up and down midway between the sprockets – the right value is in the manual), that it is **clean and lubricated**, and that no links are stiff. Worn **sprockets** have hooked teeth.
- **Belt:** cracks, damage and tension.
- **Shaft:** leaks of oil from the shaft housing.

A chain that is too loose can jump off; one that is too tight wears the gearbox and wheel bearings.

### Stand

The **side stand** (and the centre stand) should return **fully** and stay up when folded. Many motorcycles have a safety switch that stops the engine if you put it in gear with the side stand down.

### Mirrors

Firmly fixed, clean and adjusted so you see behind you past your own arms.

### Bearings

- **Steering head bearing:** with the front wheel lifted, the handlebars should turn smoothly from lock to lock without notches; with the front brake on, rock the motorcycle back and forth – you should feel no knocking at the head.
- **Wheel bearings and swingarm:** with the wheel lifted, try to move it **sideways** – there should be no play.

### Suspension and shock absorbers

Push down on the front and rear: the motorcycle should go down and come back **once**, smoothly, without bouncing. Look for **oil leaks** on the front fork tubes and the rear shock absorber.

### Engine oil

Check the level as the manual describes – often in a **sight glass** with the motorcycle **upright** on level ground, or with a **dipstick**.

### Kill switch

Start the engine and switch the kill switch to **off** – the engine should stop at once.

## Riding with faults

You may never ride in a way that is unsafe for the conditions [[src:tf|3:14]], and a motorcycle with faults in **brakes, tyres, steering** or **lights in darkness** is dangerous. Get it repaired before you ride.

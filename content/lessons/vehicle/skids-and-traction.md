## Grip

The curriculum asks you to explain how **road conditions** and the **laws of nature** affect the car, and what **steerability and stability** mean [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:2]].

Everything the car does needs **grip** ([[term:vaggrepp|sv]]) between tyres and road. Grip depends on:

- the **road surface** – dry asphalt, wet, gravel, snow, ice,
- the **tyres** – type, tread depth and pressure [[src:ts-tyres]],
- the **load** on each wheel,
- your **speed** and how **suddenly** you steer, brake or accelerate.

::: key Grip is shared
A tyre has a limited amount of grip. If you use it for **braking**, less is left for **steering** – and the other way round. Braking hard **in** a bend is therefore much riskier than braking **before** it.
:::

On **ice** the grip can be only a small fraction of the grip on dry asphalt. The most slippery ice is often ice with **water on it** – near 0 °C. See [[lesson:winter-driving]].

## Weight transfer

When you **brake**, the car's weight shifts **forward**: the front wheels press harder and grip better, the rear wheels get lighter. When you **accelerate**, the weight shifts **backward**. In a **bend**, the weight shifts to the **outer** wheels. That is why sudden movements can make a wheel pair lose grip.

## Understeer and oversteer

| | Understeer | Oversteer |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | The **front** wheels slide. The car goes **straighter** – wider – than you steer. | The **rear** wheels slide. The back swings out and the car **turns more** than you steer, or spins. |
| Common cause | Too much speed into a bend, steering harder and harder | Lifting off or braking hard in a bend, accelerating hard in a rear-wheel-drive car |
| What to do | **Ease off** the accelerator, **reduce the steering angle** a little so the tyres can grip again, and look where you want to go | **Look and steer** where you want to go – **steer against** the skid. Do not brake hard. |

::: warning Prevention is the only safe method
Recovering from a skid needs space, practice and luck. Adapt your speed **before** the bend, keep a long distance, and avoid sudden movements on slippery roads. The **skid training** in part 2 of the risk education shows how little grip there is – see [[lesson:risk-education]].
:::

## Front-, rear- and four-wheel drive

The curriculum also asks you to explain how different **drive systems** affect handling [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:2]]:

- **Front-wheel drive** – the front wheels pull. Usually stable; tends to **understeer** if you go too fast into a bend.
- **Rear-wheel drive** – the rear wheels push. Too much power in a bend or on a slippery road can make the rear slide – **oversteer**.
- **Four-wheel drive** – all wheels drive. Better **traction when accelerating** on snow and gravel. But it does **not** help you **brake or steer** better – it can give a false feeling of safety.

## Aquaplaning

At speed on a wet road, a **film of water** can build up under the tyres so they lose contact with the road – **aquaplaning** ([[term:vattenplaning|sv]]). The risk grows with **higher speed**, **worn tyres** and **deeper water** – for example in **ruts**.

Signs: the steering suddenly feels **light**, the engine revs may rise. What to do: **ease off the accelerator**, hold the wheel steady, do **not** brake hard or steer sharply until the tyres grip again. See [[lesson:darkness-and-weather]].

## ESC – the anti-skid system

**ESC** (*Electronic Stability Control*, in Swedish *antisladdsystem*) senses when the car starts to skid and brakes individual wheels – and may reduce engine power – to keep the car on course. A flashing ESC light on the dashboard means the system is working: **the road is slippery – slow down**.

According to Trafikverket's research summary, ESC reduced single-vehicle, head-on and overtaking crashes with injuries by about **31 %** – and by about **41 %** for such crashes where someone was **killed or seriously injured**. The effect on these injury crashes was about 23 % on dry roads, **56 % on wet roads** and **49 % on ice and snow** [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]].

ESC cannot change the laws of physics. If you go too fast for the grip, the car will still leave the road. The law always requires you to keep a speed at which you keep control [[src:tf|3:14]].

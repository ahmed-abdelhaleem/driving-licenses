## Ways to slow a car down

The curriculum says you must be able to use **different ways to brake a car** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:2]]:

- **The foot brake** (service brake) brakes **all four wheels**. It is hydraulic: the pedal pushes **brake fluid** that presses the brake pads against discs (or shoes against drums). A **brake servo** helps you press – it works when the engine is running, so the pedal feels much heavier if the engine stops.
- **The parking brake** holds the car still when parked. It is too weak for normal braking.
- **Engine braking** – lifting off the accelerator with a gear engaged lets the engine slow the car. See [[lesson:manual-gearbox]] and [[lesson:automatic-gearbox]].
- **Regenerative braking** – in electric and hybrid cars the electric motor works as a generator and slows the car while charging the battery. See [[lesson:electric-and-hybrid]].

## Overheated brakes

On a **long descent**, brakes used all the time get very hot and can lose much of their effect (*brake fade*). Use a **lower gear** so the engine helps, and brake firmly in short periods instead of all the time. Sign [[sign:A3|A3]] warns of steep descents.

::: tip Wet brakes
After driving through deep water the brakes may be wet and **less effective**. Test them gently at the first safe opportunity, and if they are weak, brake lightly while driving slowly to dry them [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 121]].
:::

## ABS – anti-lock brakes

If you brake very hard, the wheels can **lock** and slide. A sliding wheel cannot steer the car: the car goes **straight on**, whatever you do with the steering wheel.

**ABS** (*låsningsfria bromsar*) senses when a wheel is about to lock and releases and applies that wheel's brake many times a second. The result:

- you keep the ability to **steer while braking hard**, for example around an obstacle [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 120]],
- the car stays **stable**.

::: key What ABS does not do
ABS does **not** create more grip, and you must **not assume** that a car with ABS stops in a shorter distance [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 120]]. On ice the braking distance is still very long. ABS never replaces a safe speed and distance.
:::

When ABS works, you feel the **pedal pulsing** and may hear a grinding or rattling noise. That is **normal** – keep pressing.

## Emergency braking

At the driving test you must show **efficient braking**: stopping the car in the **shortest possible distance**, from at least **[[fact:drivingTest.brakeSpeedB]]** [[src:tv-driving-b]].

::: steps
1. **Brake as hard as you can – immediately.** Do not hesitate or "test" the brake first. With ABS, press firmly and **keep the pressure** [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 120]].
2. In a **manual** car, press the **clutch down** at the same time, so the engine does not stall.
3. **Keep the pressure** on the brake pedal all the way to a stop, even when the pedal pulses (ABS).
4. **Look where you want to go** and steer around the danger if you need to – ABS lets you.
5. When stopped, turn on the **hazard lights** if you are standing where others may run into you.
:::

**Without ABS** (older cars): brake hard, but avoid braking so harshly that the wheels **lock** – locked wheels can lead to loss of control [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 118]]. If they lock, release the pedal a little until they roll again, then brake hard again – that is the only way to steer.

Many cars also have **brake assist**, which recognises a panic stop and adds braking force, and **automatic emergency braking** (AEB), which can brake by itself. According to Trafikverket's research summary, collision warning together with automatic emergency braking reduced rear-end collisions with injuries by roughly **half** in several studies [[src:tv-vehicle-safety]]. But they cannot see everything – you are still the driver. See [[lesson:safety-systems-car]].

## Signs of brake problems

- The car **pulls to one side** when you brake.
- The pedal feels **soft** or goes too far down.
- **Squealing or grinding** noises – often worn brake pads.
- The **red brake warning light** stays on – for example low brake fluid.

Have faults checked **before** you drive on. See [[lesson:safety-check-car]].

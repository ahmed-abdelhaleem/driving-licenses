## Lights are always on

When you drive on a road, the lights prescribed for the vehicle must be on. A motor vehicle that has headlights must use them, even if they are not prescribed for it [[src:tf|3:68]]. The basic choice is **dipped beam** (*halvljus*) [[src:tf|3:69]].

::: key Colours
A vehicle may only show **white or yellow** light **forwards**, and **no white light backwards** except when reversing or working [[src:tf|3:76]]. Lights may never be used so that other drivers are **dazzled** [[src:tf|3:67]].
:::

## In daylight

In daylight you may use **daytime running lights** (*varselljus*) or **front fog lights** **instead of** dipped beam. These may **not** be used at the same time – except on a **motorcycle** in daylight [[src:tf|3:71]] [[src:ts-lighting]].

On a car, if prescribed daytime running lights are used in daylight, the rear and side lights do not have to be on [[src:tf|3:68]].

::: warning Daytime running lights are not enough in bad visibility
Daytime running lights may **not** replace dipped beam in **darkness, dusk or dawn**, or when the **weather** or other conditions make dipped beam necessary [[src:tf|3:71]]. On many cars the daytime running lights do not light the rear lamps – in rain, snow or fog, a car with only daytime running lights can be almost invisible from behind. Switch to **dipped beam**.
:::

## In darkness, dusk and dawn

Use **dipped beam** or **main beam** [[src:ts-lighting]].

::: law tf 3:70
> Vid färd på väg med bil, traktor b, motorredskap klass I, tung terrängvagn, motorcykel eller moped klass I ska helljus användas, när förarens synfält med hänsyn till fordonets hastighet annars inte är tillräckligt för att fordonet ska kunna föras säkert.
>
> Helljus får inte användas
>
> 1. på sträcka där vägen är tillfredsställande belyst,
> 2. på sådant avstånd från ett mötande fordon att dess förare kan bländas,
> 3. vid möte med ett spårbundet trafikmedel eller ett fartyg som förs längs vägen, om risk för bländning kan uppstå, eller
> 4. på ringa avstånd bakom ett annat fordon.

**In English:** "When driving a car, class b tractor, class I motor implement, heavy off-road vehicle, motorcycle or class I moped on a road, main beam **must** be used when the driver's field of vision, given the vehicle's speed, is otherwise not enough to drive safely. Main beam may **not** be used 1. on a stretch where the road is adequately lit, 2. at such a distance from an oncoming vehicle that its driver can be dazzled, 3. when meeting a rail vehicle or a ship travelling along the road, if there is a risk of dazzle, or 4. at a short distance behind another vehicle."
:::

::: tip Meeting at night
Dip your lights **in good time** before the oncoming vehicle – before its driver can be dazzled. Then look towards the **right edge** of the road rather than into the oncoming lights, and slow down if you cannot see far enough with dipped beam. More in [[lesson:darkness-and-weather]].
:::

## Fog, heavy rain or snow

- In **fog** and **heavy precipitation**, you may use **front fog lights** instead of dipped beam [[src:tf|3:71]]. Dipped beam and front fog lights may not be used at the same time (except on a motorcycle in daylight) [[src:tf|3:71]].
- In dusk, dawn and darkness you may also use main beam, but in fog this **often reduces** visibility because the light reflects back at you [[src:ts-lighting]].
- The **rear fog light** (*dimbakljus*) may only be used **in fog, heavy precipitation or similarly poor visibility** – and in a way that does not dazzle the drivers behind you [[src:tf|3:72]]. Switch it off when visibility improves or when someone is close behind you.

## Stopped or parked

In darkness, dusk or dawn – and whenever the weather requires – a vehicle stopped or parked **on a road** must show its **parking lights and rear lights** [[src:tf|3:74]]. This is not needed if the road is so well lit that the vehicle is clearly seen, if it stands **outside** the carriageway and shoulder, or for a two-wheeled bicycle, moped or motorcycle parked along the edge [[src:tf|3:75]]. While stopped, no lights other than parking, rear and number-plate lights may be on [[src:tf|3:74]] – so do not leave the headlights on to dazzle oncoming traffic.

## Flashing lights

Only these flashing lights may be used on a vehicle on the road: **direction indicators**, **alarm devices** on emergency vehicles, a cycle's **rear light**, a brake light arranged as an **emergency stop signal**, and **amber warning lights** where Transportstyrelsen allows them [[src:tf|3:76]]. Transportstyrelsen explains them like this [[src:ts-lighting]]:

- **Hazard warning lights** – when your vehicle is standing still after a breakdown or accident and temporarily obstructs traffic.
- **Amber warning beacons** – used for example by road maintenance vehicles. **Slow down and be ready to stop.**
- **Blue lights and siren** – an emergency vehicle. **Make way** for it [[src:tf|2:5]].
- **Blue and red lights flashing in turn from behind** – a police signal: **pull over and stop** in front of the police vehicle [[src:vmf|7:2]].
- **Emergency stop signal** – some cars flash their brake lights or hazard lights automatically when the driver brakes hard from about 50 km/h or more.

## Summary

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Daylight, good visibility | Dipped beam, **or** daytime running lights, **or** front fog lights |
| Darkness, dusk, dawn | Dipped beam or main beam |
| Oncoming vehicle or vehicle close in front, at night | Dipped beam |
| Well-lit road at night | Dipped beam (main beam forbidden) |
| Fog, heavy rain or snow | Dipped beam or front fog lights; rear fog light only in really poor visibility |
| Parked on an unlit road at night | Parking lights and rear lights |

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** a motorcycle must always ride with its headlight on [[src:tf|3:68]], and in daylight it may use dipped beam, daytime running lights and fog lights **together** [[src:tf|3:71]]. Being seen is a big part of a rider's safety – see [[lesson:mc-visibility-and-position]].
:::

## The rule

::: law tf 4:10e
> Vid färd på väg med ett motordrivet fordon får föraren ägna sig åt aktiviteter som användning av mobiltelefon och annan kommunikationsutrustning endast om det inte inverkar menligt på förandet av fordonet. Föraren får inte använda denna utrustning på ett sådant sätt att han eller hon håller den i handen.

**In English:** "When driving a motor vehicle on a road, the driver may engage in activities such as using a mobile phone or other communication equipment only if it does not adversely affect the driving. The driver may not use such equipment in a way that involves holding it in the hand."
:::

So there are **two** rules:

1. You may **never hold** a phone or other communication device in your hand while you use it when driving – whether or not it affects your driving. This has applied since **1 February 2018** [[src:ts-mobile]].
2. Even **without** holding it, you may only use it if it **does not affect your driving** negatively [[src:tf|4:10e]].

The rule covers **all motor vehicles** – car, motorcycle, moped, truck and bus [[src:ts-mobile]]. It also applies to the **supervisor** during private practice, because the supervisor counts as the driver [[src:ts-mobile]]. There are exceptions only for emergency services and similar urgent work [[src:ts-mobile]].

## What is still allowed?

- **Talking hands-free** is allowed – but you are still responsible for your driving. If the call takes your focus, end it. If it rings in a difficult situation, such as overtaking or dense traffic, wait until the traffic is easier [[src:ts-mobile]].
- **Listening to music** is allowed [[src:ts-mobile]].
- Using the phone as a **navigator** in a holder is not banned, since you do not hold it [[src:tf|4:10e]] – but set the route **before** you leave [[src:ts-mobile]].

Hands-free is **not required** – and it does not remove the risk, because the **conversation itself** takes your attention from driving [[src:ts-mobile]].

## Why it matters

::: key Two seconds = 22 metres blind
If you look down for **two seconds** at **40 km/h**, the car rolls **22 metres** without your control [[src:ts-mobile]]. At 90 km/h, two seconds is 50 metres.
:::

Texting, social media, watching films, typing into the navigator, playing games or searching for music while driving are all dangerous [[src:ts-mobile]]. Distraction also takes many other forms: eating, adjusting the radio, looking at something beside the road, a lively conversation, or stress.

## How to avoid distraction

Transportstyrelsen's advice [[src:ts-mobile]]:

::: steps
1. **Prepare before you leave** – route, playlist, settings.
2. Let a **passenger** handle calls and messages.
3. Make calls and send messages **when you take a break**.
4. Use technology that lets you keep your **hands on the wheel** and your eyes on the road – for example voice control or the phone's **driving mode**.
:::

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** a rider who looks down at a phone or navigator does not only lose sight of the road – a small steering input can upset the balance. Mount any navigator so you can see it with a short glance, and stop to change settings.
:::

The general rules still apply on top of the phone rule: you must be able to drive safely [[src:tf|3:1]] and always adapt to the conditions [[src:tf|3:14]]. Read more about attention and risk in [[lesson:seeing-and-planning]] and [[lesson:attitudes-and-risk]].

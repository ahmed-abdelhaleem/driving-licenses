## Who is a pedestrian?

Of course, people who walk. But the rules for pedestrians also apply to people who [[src:tf|1:4]]:

- ski, roller-ski, skate or roller-skate,
- push or pull a **kick-sledge**, a toy vehicle or similar,
- **walk beside** and push a bicycle, moped or motorcycle, or push a pram or wheelchair,
- drive a motor vehicle **meant to be driven by someone walking** beside it,
- ride a wheelchair, or certain other small electric vehicles for people with disabilities or self-balancing ones, **at walking pace**.

## Who is a cyclist?

A **cycle** (*cykel*) is not only an ordinary bicycle. In Swedish law it includes [[src:vtdl|2]]:

1. a vehicle driven by pedals or a crank,
2. an **electrically assisted bicycle** where the motor only helps when you pedal, gives no help above **25 km/h** and has at most **250 watts**,
3. some electric vehicles for people with disabilities, and
4. small **electric one-person vehicles** built for at most **20 km/h** with a motor of at most 250 watts, or that are self-balancing – for example most **e-scooters** (*elsparkcyklar*).

Transportstyrelsen's brochure on cycle crossings adds that the rules for cyclists there also apply to riders of **class II mopeds** and to people in a wheelchair going faster than walking pace [[src:ts-bike-crossings]].

## What pedestrians must do – and why it matters to you

Pedestrians must use the **footpath** or the **shoulder**. If there is none, they use the cycle track or the carriageway, and then they should walk on the **left** side, facing the traffic, where possible [[src:tf|7:1]]. They must cross at a **pedestrian crossing** if there is one nearby, otherwise straight across, and without unnecessary delay. Before stepping onto a crossing, they must consider the distance and speed of approaching vehicles [[src:tf|7:3]] [[src:tf|7:4]].

But you cannot count on everyone following these rules. Children, older people and people with a disability need extra care [[src:tf|2:1]]. A white cane means the person has impaired sight.

## Your duties towards pedestrians

### At pedestrian crossings

Transportstyrelsen's English brochure calls a crossing with traffic lights or a police officer **supervised** (*bevakat*), and one without them **unsupervised** (*obevakat*) [[src:ts-crossings]].

| Unsupervised crossing (no lights, no police) | Supervised crossing (lights or police) |
|---|---|
| **Give way** to pedestrians who have stepped onto it or are **about to** step onto it [[src:tf|3:61]]. | **Obey the signals**, and give pedestrians who stepped out correctly time to cross – **even if you have green** [[src:tf|3:60]]. |
| You may **not overtake** just before or on it [[src:tf|3:40]]. | After **turning** at a junction, drive **slowly** and give way to pedestrians on or about to step onto the crossing [[src:tf|3:60]]. |

At **every** crossing: drive so you do not have to stop **on** it [[src:tf|3:62]], and do not stop or park on it or within **10 metres before** it [[src:tf|3:53]].

::: key "About to step out" – how do you know?
Look at the people near the crossing: someone walking towards it, standing at the kerb, looking at the traffic, or a child or dog near the edge. If you are not sure, **slow down and be ready to stop**. Giving way means slowing down in good time so they can see that you will let them cross [[src:tf|3:5]].
:::

### Other places

- On **footpaths**, other places meant for pedestrians, and when you drive out from a **property** onto the road, you must give way to pedestrians [[src:tf|3:58]].
- When you **meet or overtake** pedestrians, give them enough room and time to step aside [[src:tf|3:58]].
- When you drive past a **tram or bus** that has stopped at a stop **without a traffic island**, passing it on its right side, you must **stop** and let passengers get on and off [[src:tf|3:58]].
- On a **pedestrian street** (*gågata*) and in a **walking-speed area** (*gångfartsområde*): walking pace, no parking, and you give way to pedestrians [[src:tf|8:1]].
- Where pedestrians cross the road **without** a crossing, you must keep a **low enough speed** [[src:tf|3:15]] [[src:ts-crossings]].

## Where cyclists ride

- Bicycles and two-wheeled class II mopeds use the **cycle track** if there is one. A cyclist who is at least **15** may use the carriageway instead, with special care, where the limit is **50 km/h or lower** and it suits the destination better [[src:tf|3:6]].
- Without a cycle track, they use the **shoulder** if it is wide enough and suitable [[src:tf|3:12]], otherwise they ride as close to the **right edge** as possible [[src:tf|3:7]].
- Cyclists ride **one behind the other**, but may ride **side by side** when it causes no danger or inconvenience [[src:tf|6:1]].
- Before a junction, a cyclist may keep **right** even to turn **left**: they ride across to the far side and turn there [[src:tf|3:28]].
- Cyclists may overtake vehicles other than bicycles and mopeds **on the right** [[src:tf|3:31]].
- Children up to and including the year they turn **eight** may cycle on the **footpath** if there is no cycle track [[src:tf|3:12a]].
- Cyclists under **15** must wear a **helmet** [[src:tf|6:4a]].

## Your duties towards cyclists

::: steps
1. **On a cycle track** (when you cross it), cyclists and class II moped riders have priority [[src:tf|3:59]].
2. **At a cycle crossing** (sign [[sign:B8|B8]], designed so vehicles do not go faster than 30 km/h [[src:vtdf|2]]): **give way** to cyclists who are on or about to ride onto it [[src:tf|3:61a]].
3. **At an unsupervised cycle passage** (markings only): adapt your speed so you do not endanger cyclists on it. After **turning** or when **leaving a roundabout**, drive slowly and let cyclists on or about to ride onto it cross [[src:tf|3:61]].
4. **At a supervised cycle passage**: obey the signals, and give cyclists who rode out correctly time to cross, even on green [[src:tf|3:60]].
5. **On a cycle street** ([[sign:E33|E33]]): at most 30 km/h, adapt your speed to the cyclists, and give way when you enter it [[src:tf|8:1a]].
6. Do not overtake just before or on a cycle crossing or unsupervised cycle passage, and do not stop or park on them or within 10 metres before them [[src:tf|3:40]] [[src:tf|3:53]].
:::

::: note Equal duties mean everyone looks out
A cyclist coming **from a cycle track** onto a road must give way [[src:tf|3:21]], and at a cycle passage the cyclist must slow down and cross only when it is safe [[src:tf|6:6]]. At the same time, drivers have duties towards cyclists. Transportstyrelsen explains it like this: because both have obligations, both pay attention to each other – and that makes it safer [[src:ts-bike-crossings]].
:::

::: only car
**Opening the car door:** look in the mirror and over your shoulder before you open a door on the road side – a cyclist can be right beside you. Doors may not be opened in a way that causes danger [[src:tf|3:50]].
:::

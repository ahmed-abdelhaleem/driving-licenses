## The basic rule

::: law tf 3:1
> Fordon får inte föras av den som på grund av sjukdom, uttröttning, påverkan av alkohol, andra stimulerande eller bedövande ämnen eller av andra skäl inte kan föra fordonet på ett betryggande sätt.

**In English:** "A vehicle may not be driven by anyone who, because of illness, exhaustion, the influence of alcohol or other stimulating or sedating substances, or for any other reason, cannot drive the vehicle safely."
:::

This rule covers **every** vehicle – also a bicycle. For motor vehicles, the Traffic Offences Act adds exact limits (below).

## How big is the problem?

- In **2025**, **35 people** died in alcohol- or drug-related road crashes in Sweden – **17 percent** of everyone killed in traffic [[src:tv-alcohol]].
- Trafikverket estimates that there are **at least 15,000 trips every day** in Sweden with drivers so affected that they would be convicted of drink-driving if they were caught [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
- About **20 percent** of motor vehicle drivers killed in crashes have alcohol in their body – about **35 percent** in single-vehicle crashes [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
- Among drivers killed in 2014–2021, **cannabis (THC)** was the most common illegal drug, followed by **amphetamine** [[src:tv-alcohol]].

## What alcohol does to you

Trafikverket describes how alcohol affects almost the whole body [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]]:

| Effect | What it means on the road |
|---|---|
| **Worse vision** | The eyes work together less well, so you judge **distances** worse. Even at very low levels you are **dazzled** more easily and see worse in the **dark**. |
| **Longer reaction time** | Already at low levels – especially in complicated situations where you must quickly choose what to do. |
| **Worse coordination** | Smooth, precise movements with the steering, pedals or handlebars become harder. |
| **Tunnel vision** | The brain takes in information from a smaller field of view, so you can **miss** a pedestrian, a car from the side or a sign. |
| **Tiredness** | Alcohol has a dampening effect – you do not notice that you are getting tired. |

Alcohol affects people very differently, and even the same person differently from one time to another. **Sex, body weight, food**, what you drank and **over how long** all affect the level. Spirits give a higher level than the same amount of alcohol in wine or beer [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].

## Only time helps – and the day after counts

::: key 0.15 per mille an hour
On average, the body gets rid of about **0.15 per mille** (‰) of alcohol **per hour** – but it varies between people. **Nothing** speeds it up – not coffee, a cold shower or fresh air. Only **time** [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
:::

::: example Example
Someone has **0.6 ‰** at midnight. At the average rate, it takes about **4 hours** (0.6 ÷ 0.15) to reach zero – but if their body is slower, it takes longer. And even at zero, they may not be fit to drive.
:::

Your ability can be **clearly reduced the day after**, even when the alcohol level is zero – so avoid driving the day after you have been drinking [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].

## The legal limits

::: law tbl 4
> Den som för ett motordrivet fordon eller en spårvagn efter att ha intagit alkohol i så stor mängd att alkoholkoncentrationen under eller efter färden uppgår till minst 0,2 promille i blodet eller 0,10 milligram per liter i utandningsluften döms för rattfylleri till böter eller fängelse i högst sex månader.

**In English:** "Anyone who drives a motor vehicle or a tram after drinking so much alcohol that the alcohol concentration during or after the journey is at least 0.2 per mille in the blood or 0.10 milligrams per litre in exhaled air is convicted of drink-driving and sentenced to fines or prison for at most six months."
:::

| Offence | When | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **Drink-driving** ([[term:rattfylleri|sv]]) | At least **0.2 ‰** in the blood or **0.10 mg/l** in the breath. Or **any** narcotic substance left in the blood (unless taken as prescribed). Or so affected by alcohol or **another substance** that you cannot drive safely – whatever the level [[src:tbl|4]] | **Fines** or **prison up to 6 months** [[src:tbl|4]] |
| **Aggravated drink-driving** ([[term:grovt-rattfylleri|sv]]) | Especially if you had at least **1.0 ‰** in the blood or **0.50 mg/l** in the breath, were otherwise **considerably affected**, or your driving meant an **obvious danger** to traffic safety [[src:tbl|4a]] | **Prison up to 2 years** [[src:tbl|4a]] |

Notice the words **"during or after the journey"** – the level measured after you have stopped driving also counts. A vehicle used in a drink-driving offence can be **confiscated** if it is needed to prevent more offences [[src:tbl|7]].

::: only motorcycle
The same limits apply to motorcycles – they are motor vehicles. Alcohol weakens **balance and coordination**, and on two wheels you need them all the time.
:::

## Drugs: zero tolerance

For narcotics there is **zero tolerance**: it is drug-driving if there is **any** narcotic substance left in your blood during or after driving – unless you took it as **prescribed by a doctor** [[src:tbl|4]] [[src:tv-dui-law]]. Drugs such as amphetamine, cannabis, heroin and cocaine all affect the nervous system and can cause tiredness, drowsiness, poor attention or euphoria [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].

It does not matter whether you **feel** affected – what counts is whether the substance is **in your blood**.

## Medicines – your own responsibility

Many medicines can affect your **attention** and **reaction time**, for example medicines for **pain, sleep problems, anxiety, depression, epilepsy, allergy** or **cough** [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].

::: steps
1. **Ask** the doctor or pharmacy whether the medicine affects driving – they have a duty to tell you about medicines that are dangerous in traffic [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
2. Use **only your own** medicines, in the **dose** the doctor prescribed [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
3. **Do not drive** if you feel affected – or if people around you think you are [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
:::

::: warning Prescribed is not a free pass
Even with a prescription, you can be convicted of drink-driving if you are so affected that you cannot drive safely. Under the law, **you** are responsible for how a medicine affects you in traffic [[src:tbl|4]] [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
:::

## Police checks

- The police may carry out **breath screening tests** without any suspicion – anyone can be stopped [[src:tv-dui-law]].
- If drug-driving is suspected, the police can **examine your eyes** [[src:tv-dui-law]], and a **blood test** decides whether there is a narcotic substance in your blood [[src:tv-alcohol-effects]].
- If you clearly show signs of being affected while driving, the police **take your licence on the spot** [[src:kkl|5:7]].

## What happens to your licence

Drink-driving **almost always** leads to the licence being **revoked** [[src:tv-dui-law]] [[src:kkl|5:3]].

- **Drug-driving:** the waiting period is normally **12 months** [[src:ts-revocation]].
- **Drink-driving with alcohol:** if the licence is revoked, the waiting period is **at least 12 months** – at least **24 months** for **aggravated** or **repeated** drink-driving [[src:ts-revocation]] [[src:kkl|5:6]].
- **A warning** instead of revocation is possible by law only below **0.5 ‰** (0.25 mg/l) [[src:kkl|5:9]] – and in practice Transportstyrelsen only gives it below **0.15 mg/l** in the breath [[src:ts-revocation]].
- With a waiting period of **more than one year**, you must do the **risk education** again and pass **both the theory test and the driving test** again. **During your probation period you must always take the tests again** [[src:ts-alcolock]].

## Alcolock instead of revocation

After drink-driving with **alcohol** (not drugs), you can apply to keep driving with an **alcolock** (*alkolås*) – a device that stops the vehicle from starting unless you blow a breath sample with less than **0.1 mg/l** (0.2 ‰). You must also blow at random times during the trip [[src:ts-alcolock]].

- The condition lasts **1 year** – or **2 years** after **aggravated** drink-driving or **repeated** drink-driving within five years [[src:kkl|5:20]].
- You may only drive vehicles with **your own approved alcolock**, and only **in Sweden** [[src:kkl|5:22]]. Your licence shows code **69** [[src:ts-alcolock]].
- It costs about **20,000–40,000 kronor**, and you still get the **fine or prison sentence** [[src:ts-alcolock]].

## Never ride with an affected driver

The risk education asks you to explain what you can do so that neither **you** nor **others** drive – or ride with – a driver affected by alcohol or drugs [[src:tsfs-2012-40|5:3]]:

- **Plan the way home before** the party: a sober driver, a taxi, public transport, or staying the night.
- **Say no** to a ride with someone who has been drinking – and help a friend not to drive.
- If you have been drinking in the evening, **do not drive the next morning** without being sure you are sober and rested.

People stopped by the police on suspicion of drink- or drug-driving can be offered contact with **health care** for their alcohol or drug problems, through a way of working called SMADIT [[src:tv-dui-law]].

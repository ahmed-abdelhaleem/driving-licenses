The curriculum wants you to understand how **driver behaviour and crash risk** are linked to your **willingness to follow rules** and to factors such as **age, gender, physical limitations, personality, attitude, lifestyle, social background, education and the groups you belong to** – so that you can adapt your driving to your own risks [[src:tsfs-2011-20|5:1]] [[src:tsfs-2011-19|5:1]].

## Vision Zero

Sweden's road safety work is built on **Vision Zero** (*Nollvisionen*): **no one should be killed or seriously injured in traffic**. The Riksdag (Swedish parliament) decided on it in **1997**. The current target is at most **133 deaths** in road traffic in **2030** – half the average of 2017–2019 [[src:tv-vision-zero]].

Roads, vehicles and speed limits are built to protect you – but the system only works if drivers **follow the rules**.

## Who is killed in Swedish traffic?

The official statistics for **2025** [[src:trafa-2025]]:

- **203 people** were killed – the lowest number since the end of the Second World War.
- **71 percent** of them were **men** (144 of 203).
- **73 percent** died **outside built-up areas** – most often on roads with **70 or 80 km/h** limits.
- **25 percent** died in **single-vehicle crashes** with motor vehicles – most often in cars, followed by motorcycles.
- Counted per inhabitant (average 2016–2025), the risk of being killed is highest for people **75 and older**, followed by **young adults aged 18–24**.
- Among young adults who are killed, the **car** is by far the most common way of travelling.

## Why young and new drivers?

The risk education asks you to know the differences in crash statistics between **age groups** and between **men and women**, and how risks are affected by **personality, lifestyle, group membership and driving experience** [[src:tsfs-2012-40|5:3]]. In plain words:

| Factor | Why it adds risk |
|---|---|
| **Little experience** | Handling the vehicle still takes much of your attention, so less is left for reading the traffic – hazards are seen later. |
| **Over-confidence** | Many new drivers believe they drive better than they do. The curriculum wants you to build a **realistic picture of your own ability** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:1]]. |
| **Seeking excitement** | Speed, "playing" with the vehicle and showing off. The curriculum asks how to avoid using the vehicle to **seek thrills** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|5:2]]. |
| **Tiredness** | About **30 percent** of drivers aged 20–24 say they have fallen asleep or nearly fallen asleep at the wheel in the past year [[src:vti-fatigue]]. See [[lesson:fatigue]]. |

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** Trafikverket's analyses of fatal motorcycle crashes show that only about **three in ten** riders were judged to have kept to the speed limit [[src:tv-mc-safety]]. Speed is the factor you control most.
:::

## Group pressure and passengers

The curriculum asks how **group pressure** arises and how it affects drivers [[src:tsfs-2011-20|5:2]], and that you can judge your own ability to **resist group pressure** and **impulsive actions** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|5:3]].

- **You are the driver** – you decide the speed and how you drive, not the passengers.
- Friends who cheer you on, a loud car, music and phones all take attention from the road. Ask for **calm** in the car.
- As a **passenger**: say clearly if the driver drives dangerously – and get out at the next safe place if they do not listen. Never ride with a driver who has been drinking – see [[lesson:alcohol-and-drugs]].

## Emotions, stress and distraction

**Stress, anger, being late** or a bad day make people take more risks – the risk education names passengers, **mobile phones, stress** and **play** as factors that affect road safety [[src:tsfs-2012-40|5:3]]. If you notice you are angry or stressed:

- **Let it go** – you cannot control other drivers, only yourself.
- **Leave earlier** so you are not tempted to hurry.
- Put the phone away – see [[lesson:phones-and-distraction]].

## Following the rules

The curriculum asks how a **lack of willingness to follow rules** affects safety and the environment [[src:tsfs-2011-20|5:2]]. Rules such as speed limits, keeping distance and giving way are there because each one prevents a type of crash. Breaking them also has consequences for your licence – see [[lesson:consequences]].

::: key Your choice
A crash is rarely just bad luck. Speed, distance, alcohol, tiredness and attention are **choices** – and they are yours.
:::

## Why it exists

Before you may take the tests for a car or motorcycle licence, you must complete a **risk education** (*riskutbildning*) [[src:kkl|3:4b]]. It is about the situations where new drivers are most often hurt: alcohol and drugs, tiredness, speed and slippery roads.

::: law kkl 3:4b
> Förarprov för att få behörigheten A1, A2, A eller B får avläggas endast om sökanden har genomgått en utbildning om sådan körning som är förenad med risker (riskutbildning).

**In English:** "A licence test for category A1, A2, A or B may only be taken if the applicant has completed training about driving that involves risks (risk education)."
:::

Trafikverket checks that **both parts are done and valid** when you arrive for the theory test [[src:tv-theory-b]].

## Part 1 – alcohol, drugs, tiredness and other risks

Part 1 (often called *riskettan*) is a classroom course about alcohol and other drugs, tiredness, and other risky behaviour [[src:ts-risk-car]]. For cars, the official course plan says you should be able to describe how alcohol and drugs affect the body and your driving, how to avoid driving – or riding with someone – under the influence, how sleep and tiredness affect you, and how attitudes, passengers, mobile phones and stress affect safety [[src:tsfs-2012-40]].

## Part 2 – speed, safety and difficult conditions

Part 2 (often called *risktvåan*) is practical and covers speed, safety and driving in different road and weather conditions [[src:ts-risk-car]] [[src:ts-risk-mc]].

::: only car
**For cars, part 2 includes a skid track (*halkbana*).** On a slippery surface you feel how much longer it takes to stop and how quickly a car can slide. The rules for the course say the surface should be so slippery that full braking from 45–70 km/h takes about 80 metres [[src:tsfs-2012-40]]. Part 2 should be taken near the end of your training [[src:tsfs-2012-40]].
:::

::: only motorcycle
**For motorcycles, part 2 includes a manoeuvre track (*manöverbana*)** [[src:ts-risk-mc]]. You ride a two-wheeled motorcycle and must wear a helmet and protective gear: clothes of leather or other strong material, goggles or a visor, strong boots, gloves and a back protector, all without damage [[src:ts-risk-mc]].

For category A (and A2), the motorcycle you use must have a net power of at least 20 kW; with a combustion engine it must be at least 245 cm³, and an electric one must have at least 0.15 kW per kg [[src:ts-risk-mc]].
:::

::: key Key facts
- Valid **until the day you get your licence, but at most [[fact:risk.validity]]** [[src:ts-risk-car]].
- The **car course does not count for a motorcycle**, and the motorcycle course does not count for a car [[src:ts-risk-car]] [[src:ts-risk-mc]].
- If you study at a driving school, the school books you on the course. If you practise privately, you contact a risk education provider yourself. The provider reports to Transportstyrelsen that you have done it [[src:ts-risk-car]].
- For cars, each part lasts at least **[[fact:risk.minMinutes]]** of teaching, not counting breaks, with at most 16 students per course [[src:tsfs-2012-40]].
:::

## When you do not need it

::: only car-automatic
**Removing code 78.** No risk education is needed for the driving test that removes the automatic condition [[src:kkl|3:4b]] [[src:ts-faq]].
:::

::: only motorcycle
**Moving up a category.** If you already have A2, you do not need a new risk education for the A test. If you have A1 and did the motorcycle risk education when you took it, you do not need it again either – unless you got A1 before 1 November 2009 [[src:ts-faq]] [[src:ts-a]].
:::

::: warning If you lose your licence
The risk education stops being valid on the day you get your licence. If your licence is later revoked and you must take new tests, you must also do the risk education again [[src:ts-faq]].
:::

The curriculum requires you to explain the **environmental requirements** for your vehicle, and how its **design and functions** affect safety and the environment [[src:tsfs-2011-20|2:2]] [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]].

## What comes out of a vehicle

| What | Where it comes from | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon dioxide** (CO₂) | Burning fuel in the engine | The main greenhouse gas from traffic – **climate change** |
| **Nitrogen dioxide** and **organic substances** | The exhaust | Harmful to **health**; they also help form **ground-level ozone** |
| **Particles** | The exhaust **and** wear of **brakes, tyres and the road surface** | Harmful to **health**, especially in towns |
| **Noise** | Engine, exhaust, and **tyres on the road** | Disturbs sleep and concentration, and can harm health |

Transportstyrelsen describes exhaust as containing, among other things, **particles, nitrogen dioxide and organic substances** that contribute to **ground-level ozone**, and notes that traffic also releases **wear particles** from brakes, tyres and the road surface [[src:ts-air]]. Cleaner engines have cut exhaust emissions a lot, but many people live in towns, so exposure is still high [[src:ts-air]].

## Climate

Road traffic causes **most** of the greenhouse gas emissions from domestic transport in Sweden. The national target is for these emissions to fall by **at least 70 percent by 2030** compared with 2010 [[src:ts-climate]].

How large the emissions are depends on four things [[src:ts-climate]]:

1. **How much we drive.**
2. How much **biofuel** is used.
3. How **energy-efficient** the vehicles are.
4. How many vehicles are **electric**.

As a driver you affect all four – by choosing when to drive, what vehicle and fuel to use, and **how** you drive. See [[lesson:eco-driving]].

## Particles and studded tyres

The levels of inhalable particles (**PM10**) are high in some larger towns. They can be reduced by reducing the **wear between tyres and road surface** – which is one reason why Transportstyrelsen works on requirements for **studded tyres** [[src:ts-air]]. Studded tyres also make more **noise** [[src:ts-noise]].

[[signs:C44]]

A municipality can **ban studded tyres** on a certain street, stretch of road or area through local traffic regulations [[src:tf|10:2]]. The ban is shown with sign [[sign:C44|C44]]. Where it applies, you may not drive a motor vehicle with studded tyres – choose **non-studded winter tyres** if you often drive there. See [[lesson:tyres]].

## Noise

Noise is the environmental problem that affects the **most people** in Sweden. Almost **20 percent** of the population – about **2 million people** – are exposed to traffic noise above **55 dBA** outside their homes, and **road traffic** is by far the largest source. Noise disturbs people and can harm their sleep, learning and performance; long-term exposure has been linked to a higher risk of **heart and circulatory disease** [[src:ts-noise]].

- At **low speeds**, the **engine** makes most of the noise – here electric vehicles are much quieter.
- At **higher speeds**, the noise from **tyres on the road** dominates [[src:ts-noise]].

You reduce noise by driving **smoothly**, avoiding **high engine speed** and hard acceleration – especially in built-up areas and at night – and by choosing **quieter tyres**. New tyres for cars must meet rules on **rolling noise** (the noise from the tyre on the road) – studded winter tyres and retreaded tyres are exempt [[src:ts-noise]].

## Emission standards – the Euro classes

New vehicles must meet **EU limits** for exhaust emissions, set in steps called **Euro classes** (for example Euro 5 and Euro 6). Newer classes have **stricter** limits. Transportstyrelsen advises anyone who wants to be sure of being able to drive everywhere in towns to choose a vehicle with the **latest Euro class** [[src:ts-zones]].

This matters most in **environmental zones** (*miljözoner*) – areas where a municipality has decided that only cleaner vehicles may drive [[src:ts-zones]]:

[[signs:E31-2,E31-3]]

- **Class 2:** cars, light buses and light trucks. **Petrol** vehicles must meet at least **Euro 5**, **diesel** vehicles **Euro 6** [[src:ts-zones]].
- **Class 3:** the strictest. Only **electric**, **fuel-cell** (hydrogen) and **gas** vehicles – with Euro 6 for gas vehicles. Heavy plug-in hybrids meeting Euro VI are also allowed [[src:ts-zones]].
- **Class 1:** heavy trucks and buses only [[src:ts-zones]].

There are exceptions, for example for vehicles used for **special transport services** (*färdtjänst*) and vehicles whose driver or passenger has a **parking permit for people with disabilities** [[src:ts-zones]]. More about the signs and rules in [[lesson:special-areas]].

::: only motorcycle
**Motorcycles** are not covered by the environmental zone rules – the classes apply to cars, buses and trucks [[src:tf|4:22]]. But new motorcycles must meet the EU's environmental requirements – **Euro 5** for new types from 1 January 2020 – and have on-board diagnostics that monitor the emission control system [[src:eu-168-2013]].
:::

## Fuels and energy

Besides petrol and diesel, vehicles can run on **electricity**, **biofuels**, **HVO** (renewable diesel), **biogas** and **hydrogen** [[src:ts-climate]]. Always use a fuel your vehicle is made for – the owner's manual tells you. Electric vehicles have **no exhaust** from the vehicle itself, but they still cause wear particles from tyres, brakes and the road.

## Washing your vehicle

Do **not** wash your vehicle in the street or on the driveway. The dirty water runs through storm drains straight into **streams, lakes or the sea**. It contains **oil**, detergents and **asphalt residues** that can seriously harm plants and animals [[src:hbg-wash]].

::: steps
1. Use a **car wash with water treatment**, or a **self-service wash hall** with **sludge and oil separators** connected to the sewage treatment plant [[src:hbg-wash]].
2. Choose **eco-labelled** car care products (for example the Nordic Swan label) [[src:hbg-wash]].
3. If you use a degreaser, choose one **without aromatic substances** – they are harmful to both people and the environment [[src:hbg-wash]].
:::

Local rules differ between municipalities – check what applies where you live.

## Looking after the vehicle

A well-maintained vehicle is better for the environment: the **right tyre pressure** and regular **service** save fuel [[src:ts-eco]], an engine that runs properly has cleaner exhaust, and an **oil leak** pollutes the ground and water. Check under the vehicle where it has been parked, and get leaks fixed. See [[lesson:safety-check-car]] and [[lesson:mc-safety-check]].

## Why it matters – also on the test

How you drive has a big effect on **emissions and noise**. Transportstyrelsen says that eco-driving (*sparsam körning*) is part of the curriculum for **all licence categories**, is tested in **both the theory test and the driving test**, and that a driving technique giving **lower fuel consumption** is part of the examiner's overall assessment of your driving [[src:ts-eco]]. The curricula for both cars [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:2]] and motorcycles [[src:tsfs-2011-19|3:2]] require you to use a driving technique that gives **low fuel consumption**.

According to Trafikverket, eco-driving saves on average **7 percent** of the fuel – typically **4–10 percent**, sometimes more [[src:em-eco]]. Transportstyrelsen says a private driver can save **several thousand kronor a year** [[src:ts-eco]].

## The nine steps

These are Trafikverket's nine steps of eco-driving, as published by the Swedish Energy Agency [[src:em-eco]]:

::: steps
1. **Plan ahead – avoid stopping.**
2. **Change up early** and drive in as **high a gear** as possible.
3. Drive with an **even accelerator** and **keep to the speed limits**.
4. Use **engine braking**.
5. **Do not idle.**
6. **Roll** downhill, and keep an **even accelerator** uphill.
7. Use the **engine heater** (*motorvärmare*) in cold weather.
8. **Service** the vehicle regularly and **check the tyre pressure** at least every other time you fill up.
9. **Remove the roof box** and anything else that increases resistance.
:::

Below, each step is explained.

## 1. Plan ahead – avoid stopping

Every time you stop and accelerate again, the energy you used to reach your speed is lost as heat in the brakes. Look **far ahead**. If a traffic light turns red or a queue builds up, **release the accelerator early** and let the vehicle roll – often the light changes or the queue moves before you need to stop. Keeping a good distance to the vehicle in front also makes it easier to drive smoothly.

## 2. Change up early

::: only car-manual
Change up **early**, at fairly low engine speed, and use the **highest gear** the engine can pull without struggling. Many cars have a **gear-shift indicator** on the dashboard that suggests when to change up. See [[lesson:manual-gearbox]].
:::

::: only car-automatic
In an automatic, you control the gear changes with the **accelerator**: press it **gently**, and the gearbox changes up early. Pressing hard makes it hold low gears longer – or change down (kick-down). See [[lesson:automatic-gearbox]].
:::

::: only motorcycle
Change up **early** and avoid riding at **high engine speed** when you do not need the power. High revs use more fuel and make more **noise**.
:::

## 3. Even speed – and keep to the limits

Speeding up and slowing down again and again uses more fuel than an even speed. And **higher speed costs a lot**:

::: key Speed and fuel
For every **10 km/h** faster you drive above about **70–75 km/h**, fuel consumption goes up by **0.5–1.0 decilitre per Swedish mile** (*mil* = 10 km) [[src:ts-eco]].
:::

The main reason is **air resistance**, which grows with the **square** of the speed – twice the speed gives four times the air resistance.

## 4. Engine braking

Release the accelerator **early** and let the engine slow the vehicle, instead of driving on and then braking hard. You use the brakes less, and save energy and brake wear. In an electric or hybrid car, the motor **recovers energy** when you release the accelerator (regenerative braking).

## 5. Do not idle

An idling engine uses fuel without taking you anywhere, and the exhaust ends up where people are – outside homes, schools and shops.

Many municipalities have **local rules** that limit idling. In **Karlskoga**, for example, the engine of a stationary vehicle may run outdoors for **at most one minute**. The exceptions are when **traffic** makes you stop, for example in a queue, and when the engine powers **other equipment** on the vehicle – but **not** heating or cooling the vehicle itself [[src:karlskoga-idling]]. The rules differ between municipalities, so check where you live – and do not warm up the engine by letting it idle.

## 6. Roll downhill, even accelerator uphill

Use the slope: release the accelerator before the top of a hill and **let the vehicle roll** down in gear. Uphill, keep an **even** pressure on the accelerator and accept that the speed drops a little, instead of pressing hard to keep it.

## 7. Engine heater – a warm start

::: only car
A **warm engine uses less fuel** than a cold one and lets out less carbon dioxide and fewer other harmful substances. Starting warm can cut the fuel used at the start by **up to one decilitre**, and the heat also reduces wear on the engine and the exhaust cleaning system. An engine heater is worth using at temperatures up to **+10 °C** [[src:em-heater]].

| Outdoor temperature | Switch the engine heater on |
|---|---|
| **Colder than −15 °C** | **1.5 hours** before you leave |
| **Around 0 °C** | **1 hour** before |
| **Up to +10 °C** | **30 minutes** before |

Do not run it longer than needed: if it is left on all night, the electricity will probably cost more than the fuel you save. A **timer** or a programmable heater makes it easy. A **cabin heater** usually uses about **three times** as much electricity as an engine heater [[src:em-heater]].
:::

::: only motorcycle
Motorcycles rarely have engine heaters, but the principle is the same: an engine and tyres work best when warm. Ride **gently** for the first kilometres instead of letting the engine idle to warm up.
:::

## 8. Service and tyre pressure

A vehicle that is **serviced** and has the **right tyre pressure** uses less fuel. Check the pressure **at least every other time you fill up** [[src:em-eco]]. Too low pressure also makes the vehicle handle worse and wears the tyres faster – see [[lesson:tyres]].

**Energy-efficient tyres** (a good rating on the EU tyre label) can save about **5 percent** of the fuel; together with eco-driving, about **12 percent** [[src:em-eco]].

## 9. Less load and air resistance

A **roof box** or **roof rack** adds a lot of air resistance. Take them off when you do not need them [[src:ts-eco]]. Do not carry heavy things you do not need. On a motorcycle, large top boxes and side bags also add resistance.

## Before you drive – plan the trip

The curriculum asks you to explain how your choice of **means of transport**, **travel companions** and **route** affects road safety and the environment [[src:tsfs-2011-20|4:2]] [[src:tsfs-2011-19|4:2]]. Ask yourself:

- Do I need to drive at all – could I walk, cycle or go by public transport?
- Can I **combine errands** into one trip, or **share** the car?
- Can I go at a time **without queues**?

**Short trips** are the worst: the engine is cold for a large part of the way, and a cold engine uses more fuel and pollutes more [[src:em-heater]].

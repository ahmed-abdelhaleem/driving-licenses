## Take the warning signs seriously

[[signs:A19-1,A19-2,A19-5,A19-7]]

The Police's advice is simple: **take wildlife warning signs seriously, and be especially alert at dawn and dusk** [[src:polisen-wildlife]]. Sign [[sign:A19|A19]] marks a place or stretch where there is a **special risk** of animals on or by the road [[src:vmf|2:5]] – the elk is the standard symbol; deer, reindeer, wild boar and others also exist [[src:tsfs-2019-74|2:8]].

## Reduce the risk

- **Slow down** where animals are likely – forest edges, fields, near water, and where signs warn. At a lower speed you have time to stop, and a collision is far less violent.
- **Scan the roadsides**, not just the road. In darkness, look for **reflecting eyes** and use **main beam** whenever you can.
- **One animal often means more.** Elk calves follow their mother, deer and wild boar move in groups. When one has crossed, slow down and wait for the next.
- **Reindeer** in northern Sweden often stand or walk on the road and move slowly.

## If an animal appears in front of you

::: steps
1. **Brake hard** – with ABS you can steer at the same time.
2. If you cannot stop, steer **behind** the animal if it is moving across, rather than in front of it – but only if there is room and no oncoming traffic.
3. **Avoid violent swerving** into the oncoming lane or off the road – that can be worse than hitting a small animal.
4. **Sound the horn** and flash the lights if you have time.
:::

An **elk** is tall and heavy: its body hits the windscreen and roof of a car. It is one of the most dangerous animals to hit.

## After a collision – what the law requires

::: law polisen-wildlife
> Har ett djur av arterna björn, varg, järv, lo, älg, hjort, rådjur, utter, vildsvin, mufflonfår eller örn varit inblandat i en sammanstötning med ett motorfordon, är fordonets förare skyldig att snarast möjligt märka ut olycksplatsen och underrätta Polismyndigheten.

**In English:** "If an animal of the species bear, wolf, wolverine, lynx, elk, deer, roe deer, otter, wild boar, mouflon or eagle has been involved in a collision with a motor vehicle, the driver is obliged to mark the place of the accident as soon as possible and to notify the Police."
:::

::: steps
1. **Stop** at a safe place, turn on the **hazard lights**, and put out the **warning triangle** if needed to warn other traffic [[src:polisen-wildlife]].
2. **Help anyone who is injured** and call **112** – also to report the animal. This applies **even if the animal does not look injured or lies dead at the spot** [[src:polisen-wildlife]].
3. **Mark the place** – preferably on the side of the road where the animal **disappeared**. Use the special marking strip (*viltolycksremsa*) or anything clearly visible, such as a plastic bag or a scarf [[src:polisen-wildlife]].
4. Be ready to tell the police: your **name and vehicle**, **where** it happened (place, road number, landmarks, GPS position if you can – the 112 app shows it), **which animal**, and whether it is still there or which way it went [[src:polisen-wildlife]].
5. **Do not follow or track an injured animal yourself.** The police contact a specially trained tracker [[src:polisen-wildlife]]. Stay reachable by phone for a while afterwards.
:::

::: warning Not reporting is a crime
Failing to notify the Police after a collision with one of these animals is an offence under the hunting regulations and can give a fine [[src:polisen-wildlife]]. You must also report if you have hit a **reindeer**, even though reindeer do not count as wild animals [[src:polisen-wildlife]].
:::

If the car is damaged, contact your insurance company. Do not put yourself in danger on the road while you wait – stand well off the carriageway, behind the crash barrier if there is one.

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** hitting even a deer can throw you off. At dusk and dawn, ride slower in wildlife areas and keep a position that gives you time to react to animals coming out of the verge.
:::

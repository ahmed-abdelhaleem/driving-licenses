The curriculum asks you to explain how **different road environments** affect driver behaviour and safety, and to adapt **speed, position and driving** to the conditions [[src:tsfs-2011-20|4:2]] [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:2]].

## In built-up areas

Many things happen close together: junctions, pedestrian crossings, cyclists, buses, parked cars, delivery vehicles and children. The general limit is 50 km/h, and many towns use **30 or 40 km/h** [[src:tf|3:17]].

- Expect people to step out between **parked cars**, and car doors to open.
- At junctions, watch for **cyclists** beside you and **pedestrians** crossing the road you turn into.
- Near **schools, playgrounds and bus stops**, children can appear suddenly. The law demands a low speed when you approach children on or beside the road [[src:tf|3:15]].
- Watch for **buses** leaving stops – where the limit is 50 km/h or lower, you must let them out [[src:tf|3:45]].

## On country roads

Speeds are higher and there is less room for mistakes. Typical dangers:

- **Head-on collisions** when overtaking or when someone drifts over the centre line.
- **Running off the road** in bends, especially at night or on slippery roads.
- **Junctions** with side roads and farm exits, where vehicles may pull out.
- **Slow vehicles** – tractors, cyclists, horse riders.
- **Animals** – see [[lesson:wildlife]].

::: key Hill crests and blind bends
You must keep a low enough speed at **hill crests**, in **sharp bends** and wherever the view is blocked [[src:tf|3:15]], and you may not overtake there unless you will not need the oncoming lane [[src:tf|3:39]]. Keep well to the right – an oncoming vehicle may be just over the crest.
:::

## Roads with a central barrier (2+1 roads)

Many Swedish main roads have a **central barrier** (often a steel cable barrier) and alternate between **one and two lanes** in each direction. The barrier separates the directions, which greatly reduces head-on crashes.

- **Overtake only where you have two lanes** in your direction. Do not start an overtake just before the two lanes end.
- When the two lanes merge into one, both drivers must **adapt to each other** with mutual consideration [[src:tf|3:44]], and a driver who sees someone wanting to move into their lane must adjust speed to help [[src:tf|3:43]]. Sign [[sign:F17|F17]] warns that the number of lanes decreases.
- There is little room to stop. If you break down, try to reach a lay-by, and put out the warning triangle where required [[src:tf|3:57]].

For motorcyclists, a central barrier means far fewer head-on crashes – Trafikverket notes that in **8 of 10** fatal head-on crashes involving a motorcyclist, the motorcycle crossed into the oncoming lane – but hitting the barrier itself is dangerous [[src:tv-mc-safety]].

## Gravel roads

- **Grip is lower** and the surface can change suddenly – loose gravel collects at the **edges** and in **bends**.
- **Braking distances are longer** – and do not assume that ABS will make them shorter [[src:uk-hc-general|rule 120]]. See [[lesson:brakes-and-abs]].
- **Stones** are thrown up by passing vehicles – keep your distance and slow down when you meet others.
- **Dust** can hide oncoming vehicles completely – slow down and wait until you can see.
- Gravel roads are often **narrow**: drive on the right, and slow down before hill crests and bends.

## Meeting heavy vehicles

A truck or bus needs much more room and longer to stop, and creates **air turbulence** and **spray** in rain or slush. When you meet one on a narrow road, slow down and keep well right. When you overtake one, remember it is long – you need a long free stretch [[src:tf|3:36]].

## Road works

Expect **narrow lanes**, **loose gravel**, people working close to traffic and **temporary speed limits** – often shown with [[sign:A20|A20]] and temporary signs. The law demands a low enough speed where road works are going on [[src:tf|3:15]]. Yellow road markings at road works apply **instead of** the white ones [[src:vmf|4:3]].

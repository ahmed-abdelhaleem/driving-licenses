## "Sorry, I didn't see you"

The curriculum for motorcycle licences specifically requires you to identify the risks that come from **other road users having difficulty seeing motorcyclists** [[src:tsfs-2011-19|3:2]]. A motorcycle is **narrow**, it can be hidden behind a windscreen pillar, a parked van or a hedge, and its **speed and distance are hard to judge** because it is small.

Trafikverket's analyses show that the **second most common** type of fatal motorcycle crash is the **junction crash** [[src:tv-mc-safety]].

::: key The classic danger
A car **turning left across your path**, or **pulling out from a side road**, because the driver has not seen you – or has seen you but judged that you were further away or slower [[src:uk-hc-mc|rule 86]]. Treat every car waiting at a junction as one that **may pull out**.
:::

## How to reduce the risk at junctions

::: steps
1. **Slow down** when you approach junctions and driveways – the lower your speed, the more time both you and the driver have.
2. **Cover the brakes** – fingers on the lever, foot near the pedal.
3. **Watch the car's wheels and the driver's head** – the wheels show movement before the car does.
4. **Change your position** in the lane so you are not hidden behind other vehicles, and so the driver sees you move.
5. **Have a plan B** – where would you go if the car pulls out?
:::

## Lane position

There is no single "right" position – choose it for three things:

- **See** – a position that gives you the best view ahead, through bends and past vehicles.
- **Be seen** – a position where you are in the mirrors and the view of other drivers, not in their blind spots [[src:uk-hc-mc|rule 88]].
- **Space** – a margin to oncoming traffic, to the edge, to parked cars and to hazards on the road surface.

In general, a position slightly **left of the centre** of your lane makes you visible in the mirror of the car in front and to oncoming traffic – but move **right** when meeting traffic on narrow roads, before hill crests and in left-hand bends where oncoming vehicles may cut the corner. Never ride in the blind spot of a truck or car beside you.

## Lights and clothing

- Ride with the **headlight on** – required [[src:tf|3:68]]. New models of motorcycles have lights that switch on automatically under EU rules [[src:eu-168-2013]], and in daylight a motorcycle may use dipped beam, daytime running lights and fog lights at the same time [[src:tf|3:71]].
- **Bright colours and reflective material** on jacket and helmet help, especially in town and at dusk – but they do not guarantee that you are seen.

## Riding in a group

Ride **staggered** (alternately left and right in the lane) on straight roads, with a good gap to the rider in front, and in **single file** in bends and on narrow roads. Everyone rides at their own pace – never feel pushed to keep up. Overtake one at a time.

## Keep an escape route

Always leave yourself **space** – in front, to the side, and a place to go if something happens. In queues, leave room to move; at traffic lights, check the mirror for vehicles approaching too fast behind you.

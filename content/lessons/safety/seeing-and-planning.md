## Why this matters

The curricula for both car and motorcycle say you must show **good scanning routines** (*avsökningsrutiner*), drive with **sufficient safety margins**, **identify risks**, **foresee** how traffic situations can develop and **judge the consequences** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:2]] [[src:tsfs-2011-19|3:2]]. The aim is to drive with such margins that you **do not end up in critical situations** at all [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:1]]. At the driving test the examiner looks at your **attention and risk awareness** [[src:tv-driving-b]].

## Look far, look wide, look again

::: steps
1. **Far ahead** – as far as you can see: traffic lights changing, brake lights several cars ahead, a junction, a bus at a stop, people near the road. The further ahead you look, the more time you have.
2. **Near** – the space you are driving into: the vehicle in front, the road surface, the lane.
3. **To the sides** – side roads, driveways, parked cars, pavements, cycle tracks.
4. **Mirrors** – regularly, and always before you slow down, change position or turn.
5. **Again** – keep your eyes moving. Staring at one point makes you miss things.
:::

::: key Look for clues, not just objects
- A **ball** rolling out → a child may follow.
- A parked car with **exhaust smoke** or someone in the driver's seat → it may pull out.
- A **bus** at a stop → people may cross in front of or behind it.
- A **gap** between parked cars → someone may step out.
- **Wheels turning** on a car at a side road → it is moving.
- Fresh **wet patches or shade** in winter → ice.
:::

## Plan: what can happen next?

For every situation, ask: **What could happen? What would I do then?** Then adjust **before** it happens:

- **Speed** – lower it where you cannot see far, or where people may appear. The law lists places where speed must be especially low [[src:tf|3:15]].
- **Position** – keep away from parked cars and the kerb where a door may open or a child may step out; leave space to cyclists.
- **Readiness** – lift your foot off the accelerator and **cover the brake** when something might happen.

## Safety margins

- **In time:** a long enough gap to the vehicle in front – see [[lesson:speed-and-stopping-distance]]. The law requires a distance that avoids running into it if it slows or stops [[src:tf|3:2]].
- **In space:** room to the side when you pass cyclists, pedestrians and parked cars, and a way out if something happens.
- **Behind you:** if someone follows too close, **increase** your own distance to the vehicle in front – then you can brake more gently and give the driver behind time to react.

## Communicate – be seen and understood

- **Signal** in good time and clearly [[src:tf|3:65]].
- **Position** the vehicle so others understand what you will do.
- **Eye contact** with pedestrians and other drivers – but do not trust a look alone; a person who looks at you has not necessarily seen you.
- **Be predictable** – smooth driving, no sudden moves.

## Blind spots of big vehicles

Trucks and buses have large **blind spots** ([[term:dodvinkel|sv]]) – especially to the **right** side and close in front. If you cannot see the driver's face in their mirror, they cannot see you. Do not stay beside a truck at a junction where it may turn right.

## Attention is limited

Everyone's attention is limited. Phones, passengers, stress, tiredness, alcohol and drugs all take attention away from the road. See [[lesson:phones-and-distraction]], [[lesson:fatigue]] and [[lesson:alcohol-and-drugs]].

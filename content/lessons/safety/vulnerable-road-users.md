## Who are they?

**Unprotected** (vulnerable) road users have no car body around them: **pedestrians**, **cyclists**, **e-scooter riders**, **moped riders** and **motorcyclists** – and also horse riders and people in wheelchairs. In a collision with a car, it is almost always they who are injured.

The law requires every road user to show **special consideration** for **children, older people, school crossing patrols** and people who clearly have a **disability or illness** that hinders them in traffic [[src:tf|2:1]].

## Speed decides the outcome

Trafikverket reports that the risk of injury for an unprotected road user over **65** is **three times higher** at **50 km/h** than at **30 km/h**, and that car fronts designed to protect pedestrians only help at impact speeds **below about 50 km/h** [[src:tv-safety-facts]]. This is why many towns use **30 km/h** where people walk and cycle.

## Children

Children are not small adults in traffic:

- They are **short** – they cannot see over parked cars, and you cannot see them.
- They find it hard to judge **speed and distance** of vehicles.
- They **act on impulse** – a ball, a friend or a dog on the other side can make them run out.
- They believe that if **they** can see you, **you** can see them.

The law requires a low enough speed when you approach children on or beside the road [[src:tf|3:15]]. Near schools, playgrounds and residential streets, expect the unexpected. Sign [[sign:A15|A15]] warns where children often cross.

## Older people

Many older people **see, hear and move more slowly**, and may need more time to cross. They can misjudge your speed. Give them **time** – never hurry a pedestrian on a crossing.

## Cyclists and e-scooter riders

- They can **wobble**, swerve round potholes and drains, or be blown sideways.
- They are hard to see at **junctions**, in your **blind spot** and in **darkness** – especially without lights.
- At junctions, a cyclist going straight on can be on your **right** when you turn right – one of the most dangerous situations. Check mirror and blind spot.
- Give **plenty of room** when you overtake; if you cannot, wait [[src:tf|3:33]].
- Cyclists may ride on the carriageway in many situations and may ride two abreast when it is safe [[src:tf|3:6]] [[src:tf|6:1]].

## Moped riders and motorcyclists

They are **small and fast**, so drivers easily **miss** them or **misjudge** their speed – the curriculum names the risk that other road users find it difficult to see motorcyclists [[src:tsfs-2011-19|3:2]]. Before you turn across traffic or pull out from a side road, look **twice** for two-wheelers. See [[lesson:mc-visibility-and-position]].

## Pedestrians at crossings and bus stops

- Give way at uncontrolled crossings to people who are on or about to step onto them [[src:tf|3:61]].
- **Never overtake** a vehicle that has stopped before a crossing – someone may be crossing in front of it [[src:tf|3:40]].
- When you pass a bus or tram that has stopped at a stop **without** a traffic island, on its right side, **stop** and let passengers get on and off [[src:tf|3:58]].

## Practical habits

- Slow down where people **walk and cycle** – residential streets, school zones, near shops and stations.
- Keep a **margin** to parked cars and the kerb.
- **Look twice** before turning – especially for cyclists and pedestrians crossing the road you turn into.
- **At night**, expect pedestrians without reflectors and cyclists without lights.

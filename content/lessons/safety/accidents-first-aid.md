The curriculum requires you to know **what measures can be taken at a traffic accident** and **first aid** [[src:tsfs-2011-20|3:2]].

## Your legal duties

::: law tf 2:8
> En trafikant som med eller utan egen skuld haft del i en trafikolycka ska stanna kvar på platsen. I mån av förmåga ska trafikanten hjälpa skadade och medverka till de åtgärder som olyckan skäligen föranleder. På begäran av någon annan, som haft del i olyckan eller vars egendom skadats vid olyckan, ska trafikanten uppge namn och adress samt lämna upplysningar om händelsen.

**In English:** "A road user who – with or without being at fault – has been involved in a traffic accident must stay at the scene. As far as they are able, they must help the injured and take part in the measures the accident reasonably calls for. If someone else involved in the accident, or whose property was damaged, asks for it, the road user must give their name and address and information about what happened."
:::

The same section also says [[src:tf|2:8]]:

- If **property** is damaged and nobody is there to receive your details, inform the **owner** or the **Police** as soon as possible.
- If someone has been **injured**, and the injury is not insignificant, inform the **Police** as soon as possible.
- If a vehicle stands where it is a danger or obstruction to traffic, have it moved at once – **but** if someone has been **killed or seriously injured**, move it only if it is a danger to other traffic, and do not remove traces that can matter for the investigation.

::: warning Leaving the scene is a crime
Leaving the scene of an accident you were involved in, and so avoiding helping or giving your name and information, is called **smitning** (hit and run). It is punished with fines or prison for up to six months – up to one year if the offence is serious [[src:tbl|5]].
:::

## Protect – alert – help

::: steps
1. **Protect the scene.** Stop where you do not create new danger, turn on the **hazard lights**, put on a **reflective vest** if you have one, and put out the **warning triangle** well before the scene so that others are warned in good time [[src:tf|3:57]]. Turn off the engines of the crashed vehicles if you can.
2. **Call 112.** Say what has happened, **where** (road number, direction, landmarks – the 112 app shows your position), how many are injured, and if anyone is trapped. Put the phone on **speaker** so you have your hands free [[src:1177-cpr]].
3. **Help the injured**, starting with those in the most life-threatening condition.
:::

## Unconscious and breathing normally

If a person does not respond but **breathes normally**, place them in the **recovery position** (*stabilt sidoläge*) – on their side, so the tongue cannot block the airway and vomit can run out. Keep them warm and watch their breathing [[src:1177-shock]].

## Not breathing normally – CPR

::: steps
1. **Check consciousness:** speak loudly, shake the shoulders [[src:1177-cpr]].
2. **Call 112** at once if they do not respond. With two of you, one starts CPR and the other calls and fetches a defibrillator if there is one [[src:1177-cpr]].
3. **Check breathing:** open the airway – one hand on the forehead, two fingers under the chin, tilt the head back – and look, listen and feel for **normal** breathing. Rare, gasping breaths are **not** normal breathing [[src:1177-cpr]].
4. **Chest compressions:** heel of one hand in the **middle of the chest**, the other on top. Press **5–6 cm** deep, **100–120 times a minute**. Do **30** compressions, letting the chest rise fully between them [[src:1177-cpr]].
5. **Two rescue breaths:** tilt the head back, pinch the nose, breathe in for about one second each, until the chest rises [[src:1177-cpr]].
6. **Continue 30:2** without pausing until the ambulance takes over or the person clearly wakes up and breathes normally [[src:1177-cpr]].
:::

If you cannot give rescue breaths, **chest compressions alone** are much better than nothing. Use a **defibrillator** (*hjärtstartare*) as soon as one is available – it gives spoken instructions and cannot harm the person [[src:1177-cpr]].

## Heavy bleeding

A large bleed can kill within minutes. Signs: strong or pulsing bleeding, a pool of blood the size of a plate that keeps growing, clothes soaked with blood [[src:1177-bleeding]].

- **Press hard** directly on the wound with straight arms, using your body weight – with a folded piece of cloth or with your bare hands [[src:1177-bleeding]].
- **Do not remove** objects stuck in the wound – press beside them [[src:1177-bleeding]].
- **Keep pressing** until the ambulance staff tell you to stop. Let the injured person lie down and keep them warm [[src:1177-bleeding]].

## Shock

After serious injuries and bleeding, a person can go into **shock**: fast weak pulse, pale cold sweaty skin, fast shallow breathing, anxiety, thirst [[src:1177-shock]].

- If conscious: lay them on their **back** with the **legs raised**. If unconscious: **recovery position** [[src:1177-shock]].
- Keep them **warm** with a blanket or jacket, keep **calm** around them, and **do not leave them alone** [[src:1177-shock]].
- Give them **nothing to eat or drink** [[src:1177-shock]].

::: only motorcycle
**An injured rider's helmet:** do not remove it unless it is necessary to keep the airway open or to give CPR. If you must, do it carefully, ideally with two people, keeping the head and neck still.
:::

::: note Your own safety first
Make sure the scene is safe before you approach – traffic, fire, leaking fuel [[src:1177-cpr]]. You cannot help anyone if you are run over yourself.
:::

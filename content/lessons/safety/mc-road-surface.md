## Two small patches of rubber

A car stands on four tyres. A motorcycle **balances** on two small patches of rubber, and it needs grip not only to brake and accelerate but also to **lean** through bends. If a car's tyres slide, the car skids. If a motorcycle's **front wheel** slides, the motorcycle usually **falls**.

The curriculum requires you to explain how the **road condition** (*väglag*) affects the motorcycle's handling [[src:tsfs-2011-19|2:2]]. Trafikverket names **even and predictable friction** and **loose gravel** among the road factors that matter for riders' safety [[src:tv-mc-safety]].

::: law tf 3:14
> Ett fordons hastighet skall anpassas till vad trafiksäkerheten kräver. Hänsyn skall tas till väg-, terräng-, väderleks- och siktförhållandena, fordonets skick och belastning samt trafikförhållandena i övrigt.

**In English:** "A vehicle's speed must be adapted to what road safety requires. Account must be taken of the road, terrain, weather and visibility conditions, the condition and load of the vehicle, and the traffic conditions in general."
:::

## The hazards to look for

| Hazard | Where and when | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Loose gravel and sand** | In **spring**, when gravel spread on the roads in winter is still left [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]]. At junctions, on the outside of bends, where gravel roads and driveways join the road, and along the edge, where dirt and gravel collect [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]] | Slow down **before** it. Ride over it **upright**, with no hard braking or sharp steering |
| **Road markings** – lines, arrows, pedestrian crossings | Especially when **wet** | Do not brake or lean hard on them. Cross them upright |
| **Metal** – manhole covers, steel plates at road works, tram and railway rails, joints on bridges | Very slippery when **wet** | Ride over upright. Cross rails at an angle of **45–90 degrees**, so the wheels cannot get caught [[src:sgi-mc|Railway tracks]] |
| **Tar repairs** – shiny black strips that seal cracks | Slippery when wet, and can be **soft** on hot days | Avoid leaning or braking on them |
| **Diesel and oil** | Where vehicles stand, turn and fill up – near **petrol stations, bus stops, roundabouts** and junctions. Spilt diesel makes the surface **very slippery** [[src:uk-hc-weather|rule 227]]. Oil from vehicles builds up in the **centre of the lane**, especially near junctions [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]] | Look for **shiny** patches on the road – they are slippery [[src:sgi-mc|Rain]]. Ride round them if you can |
| **Wet leaves and mud** | **Autumn** leaves under trees. Mud at field exits, farms and building sites | Slow down. Keep upright on them |
| **Water** | A puddle can hide a large **pothole**, and grip is worse in deeper water [[src:sgi-mc|Rain]]. Ruts in the road can fill with water | Avoid standing water and ride in the best part of the lane – on a wet road, often the tracks made by other vehicles' wheels [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]] |
| **Cold** | In spring and autumn, **bridges and shaded places** can be icy even when the air feels mild. Cold tyres grip less | Ride gently until the tyres are warm. Expect ice where the road is in shade |

::: key Rain after a dry spell
If it rains after a **dry spell**, the road can become **slippery** [[src:uk-hc-weather|rule 237]]. Ride extra gently in the first rain.
:::

## Signs that warn you

[[signs:A10,A11,A8,A27]]

Sign [[sign:A10|A10]] warns that the road **may be slippery**, and [[sign:A11|A11]] warns of **loose stones** thrown up by tyres [[src:vmf|2:5]]. [[sign:A8|A8]] warns of an **uneven road**, and [[sign:A27|A27]] of a **soft verge or a high road edge**. At [[sign:A20|road works]] expect loose gravel, steel plates, milled (grooved) asphalt and **edges** between old and new asphalt.

The signs do not cover every hazard – most gravel, diesel and wet markings have no sign. **You** must read the road.

## How to ride over a slippery patch

::: steps
1. **See it early.** Scan the road surface far ahead, not just the vehicle in front of you.
2. **Slow down before it**, while you still have good grip.
3. **Upright and smooth over it.** Brake, accelerate, steer and change gear as little and as smoothly as possible while you are on it – any sudden change in speed or direction can cause a skid [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]].
4. **Look where you want to go** – not at the hazard. The motorcycle tends to go where you look.
5. **Choose your line.** You can often pass to one side of the patch – but check the mirrors and your space first.
:::

If you have to brake on a slippery surface, use **both** brakes, gently – the **front** brake is still more effective than the rear, even there [[src:sgi-mc|Slippery surfaces]].

## Grooves, edges and wind

- **Grooves along the road**, a badly rutted surface or a metal bridge grating make the motorcycle **wander** back and forth. It feels uneasy but is generally not dangerous: stay on course, hold the controls firmly and ride straight across [[src:sgi-mc|Grooves and gratings]].
- **An edge or rail running along your path** – a seam between old and new asphalt, or tram rails – can catch the tyres if you try to edge across it. Move away far enough to cross it at a clear angle, then make a quick, decisive turn across it [[src:sgi-mc|Railway tracks]].
- **Side wind** – sign [[sign:A24|A24]] – and the air pushed by large vehicles can move a motorcycle sideways. Relax your arms and hold the motorcycle with your knees.

## Plan the ride

The curriculum asks you to know how different **road conditions** and road environments affect riding and safety, and to avoid riding in risky conditions such as **slippery roads and bad weather** when you can [[src:tsfs-2011-19|4:1]] [[src:tsfs-2011-19|4:2]]. Check the weather before you go, and remember that the first warm days of spring are often the days with the most gravel left on the roads. See also [[lesson:mc-cornering]] and [[lesson:mc-braking]].

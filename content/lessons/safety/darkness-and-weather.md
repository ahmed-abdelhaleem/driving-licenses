The curriculum asks you to explain how **different road conditions and times of day** affect driver behaviour and safety [[src:tsfs-2011-20|4:2]]. The basic rule is always the same: keep a speed at which you can stop within the distance you can see [[src:tf|3:14]], and the law specifically demands a low enough speed when **visibility is reduced** by light or weather, and where there is a risk of **dazzle** [[src:tf|3:15]].

## Darkness

In darkness you see **much less** than in daylight – especially what is **not lit** and **not reflective**: a pedestrian in dark clothes, a cyclist without lights, an animal at the roadside, a broken-down car.

::: key Can you stop within your lights?
Dipped beam only lights a limited stretch of road. At higher speeds your **stopping distance can be longer than the distance your lights show** – then you are driving "blind". Use **main beam** whenever it is allowed and needed [[src:tf|3:70]], and slow down when you must use dipped beam.
:::

- **Pedestrians and cyclists** are often seen too late. A **reflector** makes a person visible from much further away in your headlights. Expect people on country roads without pavements.
- **Animals** are most active at dusk and dawn – see [[lesson:wildlife]].
- **Keep the windscreen, lights and mirrors clean** – dirt and scratches scatter light and increase dazzle.
- **Tiredness** comes more easily at night – see [[lesson:fatigue]].

## Meeting at night

::: steps
1. **Dip** in good time, before the oncoming driver can be dazzled [[src:tf|3:70]].
2. Look towards the **right edge** of the road, not into the oncoming lights.
3. **Slow down** if you see less with dipped beam – and keep to the right.
4. After the meeting, switch back to **main beam** when it is allowed.
:::

If you are **dazzled**, slow down at once and, if needed, stop. Your eyes need some seconds to recover – during that time you see very little. Dip also when you drive **close behind** another vehicle [[src:tf|3:70]].

## Rain

- Grip is lower on wet roads, so braking distances are longer. The first rain after a dry period can be extra slippery because of dust and oil on the surface.
- Spray from other vehicles, especially trucks, can blind you for a moment.
- Use **dipped beam** – daytime running lights are not enough when the weather makes dipped beam necessary [[src:tf|3:71]].
- **Aquaplaning**: water in ruts or puddles can lift the tyres off the road. Slow down in heavy rain. If it happens, ease off the accelerator and hold the wheel steady – see [[lesson:skids-and-traction]].

## Fog

- Use **dipped beam** or **front fog lights** – not both [[src:tf|3:71]]. Main beam often makes it worse, because the light reflects back [[src:ts-lighting]].
- The **rear fog light** may only be used in **fog, heavy precipitation or similar poor visibility**, and must not dazzle drivers behind [[src:tf|3:72]].
- Slow down so that you can stop within what you can see, and increase your distance. Fog often lies in **patches** – in hollows and near water.

## Strong wind

Strong **side wind** – warned of with sign [[sign:A24|A24]] – hits hardest on **bridges**, in **openings in forests** and when you pass or are passed by **large vehicles**. High vehicles, cars with **trailers or roof boxes**, and **motorcycles** are most affected. Slow down and hold the steering firmly but relaxed.

## Low sun

A low sun – in the morning, evening and much of the Swedish winter – can blind you completely, and makes **dirty or scratched windscreens** glare. Keep the glass clean inside and out, use the **sun visor** and sunglasses (never at night), and slow down when you cannot see. Remember that the drivers **facing the sun** may not see **you**.

::: only motorcycle
**For riders:** rain makes road markings, metal covers and tram rails very slippery, and a wet visor reduces your view, especially at night with oncoming lights. Side wind can push a motorcycle across the lane – keep a position with margin on both sides.
:::
